The Quran is the final revelation from Allah to humanity, revealed to Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) over approximately 23 years. It is the primary source of Islamic law, theology, and spiritual guidance for Muslims worldwide. Muslims believe it is the literal word of Allah, preserved in its original Arabic form since the time of revelation.

## Revelation and Compilation

The Quran was revealed in two main phases. The **Makki** revelations occurred in Makkah over about 13 years before the Hijrah (migration to Madinah). These verses often focus on tawhid (monotheism), the afterlife, stories of past prophets, and moral exhortation. The **Madani** revelations occurred in Madinah over about 10 years after the Hijrah. These verses typically address legislation, social organization, rules of war, and relations with non-Muslims.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) received revelation through the angel Jibril (Gabriel). The verses were memorized by the Companions and written on various materials. During the caliphate of Abu Bakr, the Quran was compiled into a single manuscript. During the caliphate of Uthman, standard copies were produced and distributed to ensure uniformity in reading.

## Structure of the Quran

The Quran consists of **114 surahs** (chapters) arranged roughly from longest to shortest (with some exceptions). The total number of verses according to the Hafs narration is **6236 ayahs**. The Quran is also divided into **30 juz** (parts) of roughly equal length, commonly used for reading during Ramadan. Each juz is further divided into two **hizb** sections.

<Info>
  The numbering of verses can vary slightly between different narration traditions (qira'at). The Hafs narration, the most widely used today, counts 6236 verses. Other narrations may count slightly differently based on whether certain bismillahs and fragmented verses are counted separately.
</Info>

## Surah al-Fatihah (The Opening)

Surah al-Fatihah is the first surah of the Quran and is considered the greatest surah. It is recited in every unit (rak'ah) of every prayer. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said it is a conversation between the servant and his Lord.

<p className="io-arabic">
  بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ ﴿١﴾ الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ ﴿٢﴾ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ ﴿٣﴾ مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ ﴿٤﴾ إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ ﴿٥﴾ اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ ﴿٦﴾ صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ ﴿٧﴾
</p>

**Transliteration:**

1. Bismillaahi ar-Rahmaani ar-Raheem
2. Alhamdu lillaahi Rabbil 'aalameen
3. Ar-Rahmaani ar-Raheem
4. Maaliki yawmid-deen
5. Iyyaaka na'budu wa iyyaaka nasta'een
6. Ihdinas-siraatal mustaqeem
7. Siraatal-lazeena an'amta 'alayhim ghayril maghdoobi 'alayhim wa lad-daal-leen

**Meaning:**

1. In the name of Allah, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful.
2. All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds.
3. The Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful.
4. Sovereign of the Day of Recompense.
5. It is You we worship and You we ask for help.
6. Guide us to the straight path.
7. The path of those upon whom You have bestowed favor, not of those who have evoked Your anger or of those who are astray.

_(Surah al-Fatihah 1:1-7)_

## Ayat al-Kursi (The Verse of the Throne)

Ayat al-Kursi is the 255th verse of Surah al-Baqarah. The Prophet (peace be upon him) described it as the greatest verse in the Quran. It is often recited for protection and before sleeping.

<p className="io-arabic">
  اللَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الْحَيُّ الْقَيُّومُ ۚ لَا تَأْخُذُهُ سِنَةٌ وَلَا نَوْمٌ ۚ لَهُ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ ۗ مَنْ ذَا الَّذِي يَشْفَعُ عِنْدَهُ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِ ۚ يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ ۖ وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِشَيْءٍ مِنْ عِلْمِهِ إِلَّا بِمَا شَاءَ ۚ وَسِعَ كُرْسِيُّهُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ ۖ وَلَا يَئُودُهُ حِفْظُهُمَا ۚ وَهُوَ الْعَلِيُّ الْعَظِيمُ
</p>

**Meaning:** Allah, there is no deity except Him, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of existence. Neither drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep. To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Who is it that can intercede with Him except by His permission? He knows what is before them and what will be after them, and they encompass not a thing of His knowledge except for what He wills. His Kursi extends over the heavens and the earth, and their preservation tires Him not. And He is the Most High, the Most Great.

_(Surah al-Baqarah 2:255)_

## The Last Three Surahs (Al-Mu'awwidhat)

These three short surahs are known as al-Mu'awwidhat (the verses of seeking refuge). The Prophet (peace be upon him) would recite them at night before sleeping, blow into his hands, and wipe over his body for protection.

### Surah al-Ikhlas (Sincerity) - 112

<p className="io-arabic">
  بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ ﴿١﴾ قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ﴿٢﴾ اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ ﴿٣﴾ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ ﴿٤﴾ وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ ﴿٥﴾
</p>

**Meaning:** Say, He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent.

_(Surah al-Ikhlas 112:1-4)_

### Surah al-Falaq (The Daybreak) - 113

<p className="io-arabic">
  بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ ﴿١﴾ قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ الْفَلَقِ ﴿٢﴾ مِنْ شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ ﴿٣﴾ وَمِنْ شَرِّ غَاسِقٍ إِذَا وَقَبَ ﴿٤﴾ وَمِنْ شَرِّ النَّفَّاثَاتِ فِي الْعُقَدِ ﴿٥﴾ وَمِنْ شَرِّ حَاسِدٍ إِذَا حَسَدَ ﴿٦﴾
</p>

**Meaning:** Say, I seek refuge in the Lord of daybreak from the evil of that which He created, and from the evil of darkness when it settles, and from the evil of the blowers in knots, and from the evil of an envier when he envies.

_(Surah al-Falaq 113:1-5)_

### Surah an-Nas (Mankind) - 114

<p className="io-arabic">
  بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ ﴿١﴾ قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ النَّاسِ ﴿٢﴾ مَلِكِ النَّاسِ ﴿٣﴾ إِلَٰهِ النَّاسِ ﴿٤﴾ مِنْ شَرِّ الْوَسْوَاسِ الْخَنَّاسِ ﴿٥﴾ الَّذِي يُوَسْوِسُ فِي صُدُورِ النَّاسِ ﴿٦﴾ مِنَ الْجِنَّةِ وَالنَّاسِ ﴿٧﴾
</p>

**Meaning:** Say, I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind, the Sovereign of mankind, the God of mankind, from the evil of the retreating whisperer, who whispers into the breasts of mankind, from among the jinn and mankind.

_(Surah an-Nas 114:1-6)_

## Tips for Reading and Memorizing

Reading and memorizing the Quran (hifz) is a noble endeavor that requires patience, consistency, and sincere intention. Here are practical tips based on the practices of successful memorizers and classical scholars:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sincere Intention">
    Begin with the intention of seeking Allah's pleasure. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said that actions are judged by intentions. Make your goal to draw closer to Allah, not merely to accumulate verses.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Consistent Daily Amount">
    Set a realistic daily portion, even if it is just one verse or one page. The Prophet (peace be upon him) advised Abdullah ibn Amr to read the Quran in one month, and when Abdullah wanted to do more, the Prophet guided him to a moderate pace. Consistency is more important than speed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Quality Over Quantity">
    Focus on perfecting the pronunciation (tajweed) and understanding the meaning rather than rushing through pages. Revise old memorization before adding new material.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Listen to Qualified Reciters">
    Listening to skilled reciters helps internalize correct pronunciation, rhythm, and melody. Many students find that listening repeatedly to the same portion dramatically improves retention.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Understand the Meaning">
    Reading a translation or tafsir (exegesis) helps anchor verses in your memory. When you understand what you are memorizing, the words become meaningful rather than abstract sounds.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a Fixed Time and Place">
    The early morning after Fajr prayer is considered the optimal time for memorization due to mental freshness and barakah. A dedicated, quiet space minimizes distractions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Recite to a Teacher">
    Regular review with a qualified teacher ensures correct recitation and provides accountability. The classical method emphasizes oral transmission from teacher to student.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Etiquette of Reading the Quran

Muslims observe certain manners when interacting with the Quran to show reverence for the word of Allah:

- **Purification:** Perform wudu (ablution) before touching the Quran, based on the Quranic verse "None touch it except the purified" (56:79).
- **Facing the Qiblah:** When possible, sit facing the Qiblah while reading.
- **Beginning with Ta'awwudh:** Say "A'udhu billahi minash-shaytanir-rajeem" (I seek refuge in Allah from Satan, the accursed) before reading, except at the start of Surah At-Tawbah.
- **Beginning with Basmalah:** Say "Bismillahir-Rahmanir-Raheem" before each surah except Surah At-Tawbah.
- **Moderate Pace:** Do not rush. The Prophet (peace be upon him) advised reading with tartil (measured, clear recitation).
- **Reflecting on Meanings:** Pause to contemplate the verses and how they apply to your life.
- **Not Talking During Recitation:** Give the Quran your full attention.
- **Proper Handling:** Place the Quran in a respectful place, not on the floor or in unclean areas.

<Tip>
  Even if you do not read Arabic, listening to the Quran with attentiveness and reflection carries great reward. Many Muslims listen to recitation during commutes, while working, or before sleep.
</Tip>
