Hadith (plural: ahadith) refers to the recorded statements, actions, approvals, and descriptions of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him). Along with the Quran, hadith forms the second fundamental source of Islamic law, theology, and daily practice. Muslims believe that the Prophet's words and example provide divine guidance, as Allah says in the Quran: "And whatever the Messenger has given you, take it; and what he has forbidden you, refrain from it" (Surah al-Hashr 59:7).

## What Is Hadith?

A hadith consists of two main parts:

- **Matn (text):** The actual content of the narration, containing the saying, action, or description.
- **Isnad (chain of narration):** The chain of transmitters who passed the report from the Prophet (peace be upon him) down to the compiler. Each narrator is identified by name, allowing scholars to verify their reliability.

The science of hadith (ilm al-hadith) developed sophisticated methods to evaluate the authenticity of narrations. Scholars examined the integrity, memory, character, and precision of every narrator in the chain, as well as the continuity and consistency of the transmission.

## Grades of Hadith

Scholars classify hadith into several categories based on the strength of their chain and content:

| Grade | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Sahih** | Authentic. The chain consists of reliable, precise narrators with an unbroken connection to the Prophet, and the content does not contradict stronger evidence or established principles. |
| **Hasan** | Good. The chain meets most criteria of sahih but may have minor weaknesses, such as a narrator with slightly less perfect memory. Hasan hadith are accepted as evidence in Islamic law. |
| **Da'if** | Weak. The chain contains a significant weakness, such as a narrator known for poor memory, a break in the chain, or other defects. Da'if hadith are generally not used as primary legal evidence, though they may be referenced for virtues or encouragement. |
| **Mawdu'** | Fabricated. The hadith is falsely attributed to the Prophet. These are rejected entirely. |

<Info>
  The classification above reflects the mainstream scholarly tradition. Different scholars and madhhabs may apply criteria with slight variations, and some scholars are more lenient or strict in their evaluations. The major hadith collections generally include only sahih and hasan narrations.
</Info>

## The Six Major Collections (Kutub al-Sittah)

The most authoritative collections of hadith in Sunni Islam are known as the Kutub al-Sittah (The Six Books). These were compiled during the 3rd century AH (9th-10th century CE) by scholars who traveled extensively to gather and verify narrations.

| Collection | Compiler | Approximate Death | Notable Characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sahih al-Bukhari** | Muhammad ibn Ismail al-Bukhari | 256 AH / 870 CE | Considered the most authentic collection. Contains approximately 7,000 hadith after accounting for repetitions. Al-Bukhari reportedly memorized over 300,000 hadith and selected only those meeting his strict criteria. |
| **Sahih Muslim** | Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj al-Naysaburi | 261 AH / 875 CE | The second most authentic collection. Muslim organized hadith by topic and paid meticulous attention to the exact wording of narrations. |
| **Sunan Abu Dawud** | Abu Dawud Sulayman ibn al-Ash'ath al-Sijistani | 275 AH / 889 CE | Focuses on legal hadith related to fiqh (jurisprudence). Abu Dawud included some narrations with minor weaknesses when they were the only evidence on a topic. |
| **Jami' at-Tirmidhi** | Muhammad ibn Isa at-Tirmidhi | 279 AH / 892 CE | Known for including both sahih and hasan hadith, and for noting the opinions of early jurists. At-Tirmidhi was the first to systematically classify hadith as sahih, hasan, or da'if. |
| **Sunan an-Nasa'i** | Ahmad ibn Shu'ayb an-Nasa'i | 303 AH / 915 CE | Highly regarded for its rigor. An-Nasa'i compiled multiple versions, with his "al-Mujtaba" (the selected) being the most famous. |
| **Sunan Ibn Majah** | Muhammad ibn Yazid Ibn Majah al-Qazwini | 273 AH / 887 CE | Contains some narrations not found in the other five collections. Scholars have debated whether it should be replaced by the Muwatta of Imam Malik in the canonical six. |

<Note>
  The Muwatta of Imam Malik ibn Anas (d. 179 AH / 795 CE) is sometimes considered the seventh major collection. It is the earliest written compilation of hadith and legal rulings, predating the Kutub al-Sittah by nearly a century.
</Note>

## Collection of Authentic Hadith

The following hadith are well-known, widely transmitted, and authenticated by classical scholars. They are grouped by theme for easier reference.

### Foundations of Faith

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Actions Are Judged by Intentions">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "Actions are judged by intentions, and every person will have what they intended. So whoever emigrated for Allah and His Messenger, their emigration is for Allah and His Messenger. And whoever emigrated for worldly gain or to marry a woman, their emigration is for what they emigrated for."

    _(Sahih al-Bukhari 1, Sahih Muslim 1907)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Islam Is Built on Five Pillars">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "Islam is built upon five: testifying that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establishing prayer, giving zakat, fasting Ramadan, and performing Hajj if one is able."

    _(Sahih al-Bukhari 8, Sahih Muslim 16)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The Hadith of Jibril">
    Umar ibn al-Khattab said: "While we were sitting with the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him), a man with extremely white clothing and extremely black hair came. None of us recognized him, and he did not show signs of travel. He sat before the Prophet, rested his knees against his, and placed his hands on his thighs. He said: 'O Muhammad, tell me about Islam.' The Messenger of Allah said: 'Islam is to testify that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, to establish prayer, to give zakat, to fast Ramadan, and to perform Hajj if you are able.' He said: 'You have spoken rightly.' We were amazed that he asked and then affirmed. He said: 'Then tell me about iman (faith).' He said: 'It is to believe in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and to believe in divine decree, both its good and its evil.' He said: 'You have spoken rightly.' He said: 'Then tell me about ihsan (excellence).' He said: 'It is to worship Allah as if you see Him, and even if you do not see Him, He sees you.' He said: 'Then tell me about the Hour.' He said: 'The one asked knows no more than the asker.' He said: 'Then tell me about its signs.' He said: 'The slave girl will give birth to her mistress, and you will see the barefoot, naked, destitute shepherds competing in the construction of tall buildings.' Then the man left. The Prophet said: 'That was Jibril, who came to teach you your religion.'"

    _(Sahih Muslim 8)_
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### Character and Brotherhood

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="None of You Truly Believes Until He Loves for His Brother">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "None of you truly believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself."

    _(Sahih al-Bukhari 13, Sahih Muslim 45)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The Strongest Bond of Faith">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "The strongest bond of faith is to love for the sake of Allah and to hate for the sake of Allah."

    _(Sunan Abu Dawud 4681, authenticated by scholars)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The Believer Is a Mirror to His Brother">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "The believer is a mirror to his fellow believer. He protects him behind his back."

    _(Sunan Abu Dawud 4918, authenticated by al-Albani)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Removing Harm from the Path">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "While a man was walking on a road, he became very thirsty. He found a well and went down into it to drink. When he came out, he saw a dog panting and licking mud because of thirst. The man said: 'This dog is experiencing the same thirst I experienced.' So he went down into the well again, filled his shoe with water, and held it in his mouth to climb out. He gave the dog water. Allah appreciated his deed and forgave him." The people asked: "O Messenger of Allah, is there a reward for us in serving animals?" He said: "Yes, there is a reward for serving every living creature."

    _(Sahih al-Bukhari 2363, Sahih Muslim 2244)_
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### Worship and Spiritual Life

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The Five Daily Prayers">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "Whoever performs ablution properly and then prays two rak'ahs with complete presence of mind and humility, all their previous sins are forgiven."

    _(Sahih al-Bukhari 158, Sahih Muslim 226)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The Excellence of Fasting">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "Every deed of the son of Adam is multiplied: a good deed is multiplied tenfold up to seven hundredfold. Allah says: 'Except fasting, for it is Mine and I will reward it. The person leaves their desires and food for My sake.'"

    _(Sahih al-Bukhari 1904, Sahih Muslim 1151)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The Virtue of Dhikr">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "Two words are light on the tongue, heavy on the scale, and beloved to the Most Merciful: SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi, SubhanAllahil-Adheem."

    _(Sahih al-Bukhari 6682, Sahih Muslim 2694)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The Night Prayer">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "The best prayer after the obligatory prayers is the night prayer."

    _(Sahih Muslim 1163)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The Most Beloved Deeds to Allah">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) was asked about the most beloved deeds to Allah. He said: "The most beloved deeds to Allah are those that are most consistent, even if they are small."

    _(Sahih al-Bukhari 6464, Sahih Muslim 783)_
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### Knowledge and Wisdom

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Seeking Knowledge Is an Obligation">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "Seeking knowledge is an obligation upon every Muslim."

    _(Sunan Ibn Majah 224, authenticated by scholars)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The Scholar and the Seeker">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "Whoever follows a path in pursuit of knowledge, Allah makes easy for them a path to Paradise."

    _(Sahih Muslim 2699)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Wisdom Is the Lost Property of the Believer">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "Wisdom is the lost property of the believer. Wherever they find it, they have more right to it."

    _(Sunan at-Tirmidhi 2687, authenticated by scholars)_
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### Character, Manners, and Daily Life

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The Best Among You">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "The best among you are those who have the best manners and character."

    _(Sahih al-Bukhari 3559, Sahih Muslim 2321)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Smiling Is Charity">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "Your smile for your brother is charity. Your removal of stones and thorns from people's paths is charity. Your guiding of a person who is lost is charity."

    _(Sunan at-Tirmidhi 1956, authenticated by scholars)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The Mercy of the Strong">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "The strong is not the one who overcomes people by his strength, but the strong is the one who controls himself while in anger."

    _(Sahih al-Bukhari 6114, Sahih Muslim 2609)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Good Character">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "I was sent to perfect good character."

    _(Sunan al-Bayhaqi, authenticated by al-Albani; also found in Musnad Ahmad)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The Dua of the Oppressed">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "Beware of the supplication of the oppressed, for there is no barrier between it and Allah."

    _(Sahih al-Bukhari 2448, Sahih Muslim 19)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The World Is a Prison for the Believer">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "The world is a prison for the believer and a paradise for the disbeliever."

    _(Sahih Muslim 2956)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Cleanliness Is Half of Faith">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "Cleanliness is half of faith."

    _(Sahih Muslim 223)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Paradise Lies at the Feet of Mothers">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "Paradise lies at the feet of mothers."

    _(Sunan an-Nasa'i 3104, authenticated by scholars)_
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### Repentance and Forgiveness

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Allah Is More Delighted with Repentance">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "Allah is more delighted with the repentance of His servant than one of you who finds his lost camel in a barren land."

    _(Sahih al-Bukhari 2305, Sahih Muslim 2744)_
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Allah's Mercy Prevails Over His Wrath">
    The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "Allah says: 'O son of Adam, so long as you call upon Me and ask of Me, I shall forgive you for what you have done, and I shall not mind. O son of Adam, were your sins to reach the clouds of the sky and were you then to ask forgiveness of Me, I would forgive you. O son of Adam, were you to come to Me with sins nearly as great as the earth and were you then to face Me, ascribing no partner to Me, I would bring you forgiveness nearly as great as it.'"

    _(Sunan at-Tirmidhi 3540, authenticated by scholars)_
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Tip>
  When studying hadith, it is valuable to read the context and explanation (sharh) provided by scholars. A single hadith may have layers of meaning that become apparent through study of the circumstances of its revelation, the broader prophetic teaching on the topic, and the understanding of the early generations (salaf).
</Tip>
