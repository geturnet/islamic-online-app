Salah, the ritual prayer, is the second pillar of Islam and the most important daily act of worship after the testimony of faith. It is a direct connection between the servant and Allah, performed five times every day at prescribed times. The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) said: "Pray as you have seen me praying." (Sahih al-Bukhari 631)

This guide explains how to perform Salah from beginning to end. It is written for beginners who are learning to pray, for Muslims returning to regular prayer, and for anyone who wants to review the correct method. The instructions follow the most widely accepted practice across Sunni schools of thought, with notes where madhhab differences exist.

Before you begin, you may want to check today's <a href="/prayer-times">prayer times</a>, find your <a href="/tools/qibla-direction">Qibla direction</a>, or browse our collection of <a href="/dua">daily duas</a>.

## Prerequisites for Salah

Before standing for prayer, several conditions must be met. These are not mere formalities; they are part of the spiritual and physical preparation that makes Salah valid and meaningful.

### Purity of body: Wudu (ablution)

Wudu is the ritual washing of specific body parts before prayer. Allah says in the Quran:

<p className="io-arabic">يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا قُمْتُمْ إِلَى الصَّلَاةِ فَاغْسِلُوا وُجُوهَكُمْ وَأَيْدِيَكُمْ إِلَى الْمَرَافِقِ وَامْسَحُوا بِرُءُوسِكُمْ وَأَرْجُلَكُمْ إِلَى الْكَعْبَيْنِ</p>

**Translation:** "O you who have believed, when you rise to [perform] prayer, wash your faces and your forearms to the elbows and wipe over your heads and wash your feet to the ankles." (Quran 5:6)

<Steps>
  <Step title="Make the intention (niyyah)">
    Intend in your heart to perform wudu for the sake of Allah. The intention is a mental act and need not be spoken aloud.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say Bismillah">
    Begin by saying "Bismillah" (In the name of Allah). Some scholars say this is recommended, others say it is obligatory. All agree it is essential to begin with the name of Allah.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wash the hands three times">
    Wash both hands up to the wrists, starting with the right. Rub between the fingers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rinse the mouth and nose three times">
    Take water into the mouth, swish it around, and expel it. Then inhale water gently into the nostrils and blow it out. Use the right hand for the mouth and the left for the nose.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wash the face three times">
    Wash the entire face from the hairline to the chin and from ear to ear.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wash the arms to the elbows three times">
    Begin with the right arm, washing from the fingertips to the elbow. Then do the left. Ensure water reaches every part, including between the fingers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wipe the head once">
    Wet your hands and wipe over the head from front to back and back to front. Then wipe the inner ears with your index fingers and the back of the ears with your thumbs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wash the feet to the ankles three times">
    Begin with the right foot, washing from the toes to the ankle. Use your fingers to rub between the toes. Then do the left foot.
  </Step>
</Steps>

After completing wudu, it is recommended to say the following dua:

<p className="io-arabic">أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُهُ وَرَسُولُهُ</p>

**Transliteration:** "Ashhadu an la ilaha illallahu wahdahu la sharika lahu, wa ashhadu anna Muhammadan abduhu wa rasuluhu."

**Translation:** "I bear witness that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah alone, without partner, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and messenger."

### When ghusl (full bath) is required

Ghusl, the ritual bath, replaces wudu and is required after major ritual impurity. This includes after sexual relations, seminal emission, menstruation, and postnatal bleeding. Ghusl involves washing the entire body with water, ensuring it reaches every part, including the roots of the hair. For a complete guide on ghusl, consult a qualified teacher or reliable fiqh resource.

### Clean clothes and clean place

Your body, clothes, and place of prayer must be free from najasah (ritual impurity). If you are unsure whether something is impure, the default assumption is purity unless you have clear evidence otherwise. The prayer mat or area should be clean. It is recommended to use a prayer mat or a clean surface.

### Covering the awrah

The awrah is the part of the body that must be covered during prayer. For men, this is from the navel to the knees, inclusive. For women, it is the entire body except the face and hands. Loose, opaque clothing that does not reveal the shape of the body is required.

### Facing the Qibla

The Qibla is the direction of the Kaaba in Makkah. Muslims face the Qibla during every prayer. If you are unsure of the direction, use our <a href="/tools/qibla-direction">Qibla direction tool</a> or a reliable compass app. If you genuinely cannot determine the direction, pray in the direction you believe is most likely correct. If you later discover you were facing the wrong direction, your prayer is still valid.

### Prayer time

Each of the five daily prayers must be performed within its specific time window. These times are determined by the position of the sun. You can find accurate <a href="/prayer-times">prayer times for your location</a> on our site. The times are:

- **Fajr:** From dawn until sunrise
- **Dhuhr:** From when the sun passes its zenith until the shadow of an object equals its length (plus the shadow at zenith)
- **Asr:** From the end of Dhuhr time until sunset
- **Maghrib:** From sunset until twilight disappears (redness in the sky)
- **Isha:** From the end of Maghrib time until dawn

### Intention (niyyah)

Before beginning the prayer, make the intention in your heart for which prayer you are performing: Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, or Isha. The intention should also specify whether it is obligatory (fard), recommended (sunnah), or makeup (qada). The intention is a condition of the heart and should not be spoken aloud, though some scholars permit a silent verbal intention.

## The five daily prayers

Every Muslim past the age of puberty is obligated to perform five daily prayers. Each prayer consists of a specific number of obligatory (fard) units called rak'ah. There are also recommended (sunnah) prayers before and after the obligatory prayers, which the Prophet (peace be upon him) consistently performed.

The following table summarizes the fard and common sunnah rak'ah for each prayer:

| Prayer | Fard rak'ah | Common sunnah before | Common sunnah after | Total commonly prayed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fajr | 2 | 2 | 0 | 4 |
| Dhuhr | 4 | 4 | 2 | 10 |
| Asr | 4 | 0 | 0 | 4 |
| Maghrib | 3 | 0 | 2 | 5 |
| Isha | 4 | 2 | 2 | 8 |

<Info>
  Sunnah prayers are highly recommended but not obligatory. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "The most excellent prayer after the obligatory prayers is the night prayer." (Sahih Muslim 1163) There are also additional sunnah prayers such as the rawatib (regularly associated sunnah), duha (forenoon prayer), tahajjud (night prayer), and witr.
</Info>

## Step-by-step: two rak'ah prayer

This section walks through a complete two-rak'ah prayer, which is the structure of Fajr and all other prayers build upon it. The method described here is the most common practice across the Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali schools, with notes on differences where relevant.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Stand facing the Qibla (qiyam)">
    Stand upright with your feet about shoulder-width apart, facing the Kaaba in Makkah. Keep your gaze lowered to the place of prostration. This standing position is called **qiyam**.

    Place your hands. The majority view across schools is to place the right hand over the left, either below the navel (Hanafi), at the chest (Shafi'i, Hanbali), or between the navel and chest (Maliki). Some scholars raise the hands to the shoulders or earlobes when saying "Allahu Akbar" at the beginning of prayer and when rising from ruku. Others do not raise the hands except at the opening takbir. Follow the method taught by your school or local community.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say the opening takbir (takbirat al-ihram)">
    Raise your hands to your shoulders or earlobes (according to the practice you follow) and say:

    <p className="io-arabic">اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ</p>

    **Transliteration:** "Allahu Akbar"

    **Translation:** "Allah is the Greatest."

    This takbir marks the entry into prayer. From this moment until the taslim (salam), you are in the state of prayer. Ordinary speech, eating, drinking, and other worldly actions are prohibited.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Opening supplication (dua al-istiftah / thana)">
    After the takbir, it is recommended to recite the opening supplication. One of the most common versions is:

    <p className="io-arabic">سُبْحَانَكَ اللَّهُمَّ وَبِحَمْدِكَ وَتَبَارَكَ اسْمُكَ وَتَعَالَى جَدُّكَ وَلَا إِلَٰهَ غَيْرُكَ</p>

    **Transliteration:** "Subhanakallahumma wa bihamdika, wa tabarakasmuka, wa ta'ala jadduka, wa la ilaha ghairuk."

    **Translation:** "Glory be to You, O Allah, and praise be to You. Blessed is Your name, and exalted is Your majesty. There is no deity worthy of worship except You."

    Another commonly taught version is:

    <p className="io-arabic">اللَّهُمَّ بَاعِدْ بَيْنِي وَبَيْنَ خَطَايَايَ كَمَا بَاعَدْتَ بَيْنَ الْمَشْرِقِ وَالْمَغْرِبِ... (to the end of the dua)</p>

    This is the dua reported from the Prophet (peace be upon him) in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Seek refuge and basmalah">
    Say:

    <p className="io-arabic">أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيمِ</p>

    **Transliteration:** "A'udhu billahi minash-shaitanir-rajim"

    **Translation:** "I seek refuge in Allah from the accursed Satan."

    Then say the basmalah:

    <p className="io-arabic">بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ</p>

    **Transliteration:** "Bismillahir-rahmanir-rahim"

    **Translation:** "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful."

    In audible prayers (Fajr, first two rak'ah of Maghrib and Isha, Jumu'ah, Tarawih), these are recited aloud. In silent prayers (Dhuhr, Asr, third and fourth rak'ah of Maghrib and Isha, voluntary prayers), they are recited silently.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Recite Surah Al-Fatihah">
    Recite Surah Al-Fatihah, the Opening of the Book. This is obligatory in every rak'ah of every prayer. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "There is no prayer for the one who does not recite the Opening of the Book." (Sahih al-Bukhari 756, Sahih Muslim 394)

    <p className="io-arabic">بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ ﴿١﴾ الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ ﴿٢﴾ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ ﴿٣﴾ مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ ﴿٤﴾ إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ ﴿٥﴾ اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ ﴿٦﴾ صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ ﴿٧﴾</p>

    **Transliteration:** "Bismillahir-rahmanir-rahim. Alhamdu lillahi rabbil-'alamin. Ar-rahmanir-rahim. Maliki yawmid-din. Iyyaka na'budu wa iyyaka nasta'in. Ihdinas-siratal mustaqim. Siratal-ladhina an'amta 'alayhim, ghayril-maghdubi 'alayhim wa lad-dallin."

    **Translation:** "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. All praise is due to Allah, Lord of all the worlds. The Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Master of the Day of Judgment. You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help. Guide us to the straight path. The path of those upon whom You have bestowed favor, not of those who have evoked Your anger or of those who are astray." (Quran 1:1-7)

    After completing Al-Fatihah, say "Ameen" (O Allah, accept this).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Recite a short surah or verses">
    After Surah Al-Fatihah, recite any other surah or a portion of a surah from the Quran. In the first two rak'ah of every prayer, this is obligatory (fard according to some schools, wajib according to others, but universally agreed to be required). In the third and fourth rak'ah, it is not required.

    Common short surahs for beginners include:

    - **Surah Al-Ikhlas (112):** <p className="io-arabic">قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ﴿١﴾ اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ ﴿٢﴾ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ ﴿٣﴾ وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ ﴿٤﴾</p>
    - **Surah Al-Falaq (113):** <p className="io-arabic">قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ الْفَلَقِ ﴿١﴾ مِنْ شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ ﴿٢﴾ وَمِنْ شَرِّ غَاسِقٍ إِذَا وَقَبَ ﴿٣﴾ وَمِنْ شَرِّ النَّفَّاثَاتِ فِي الْعُقَدِ ﴿٤﴾ وَمِنْ شَرِّ حَاسِدٍ إِذَا حَسَدَ ﴿٥﴾</p>
    - **Surah An-Nas (114):** <p className="io-arabic">قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ النَّاسِ ﴿١﴾ مَلِكِ النَّاسِ ﴿٢﴾ إِلَٰهِ النَّاسِ ﴿٣﴾ مِنْ شَرِّ الْوَسْوَاسِ الْخَنَّاسِ ﴿٤﴾ الَّذِي يُوَسْوِسُ فِي صُدُورِ النَّاسِ ﴿٥﴾ مِنَ الْجِنَّةِ وَالنَّاسِ ﴿٦﴾</p>

    You can browse more surahs in our <a href="/quran">Quran section</a>.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Bowing (ruku)">
    Say "Allahu Akbar" and bow down, placing your hands on your knees. Keep your back straight and level, neither raising your head nor lowering it excessively. Your fingers should be spread, and your arms should not touch your sides.

    While in ruku, glorify Allah by saying three or more times:

    <p className="io-arabic">سُبْحَانَ رَبِّيَ الْعَظِيمِ</p>

    **Transliteration:** "Subhana Rabbiyal Azim"

    **Translation:** "Glory be to my Lord, the Magnificent."

    This is the minimum. You may add other supplications such as "Subhanakallahumma Rabbana wa bihamdika, Allahumma ighfirli" (Glory be to You, our Lord, and praise be to You. O Allah, forgive me).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rising from ruku (i'tidal)">
    Rise from ruku while saying:

    <p className="io-arabic">سَمِعَ اللَّهُ لِمَنْ حَمِدَهُ</p>

    **Transliteration:** "Sami Allahu liman hamidah"

    **Translation:** "Allah hears the one who praises Him."

    Then, while standing upright, say:

    <p className="io-arabic">رَبَّنَا وَلَكَ الْحَمْدُ</p>

    **Transliteration:** "Rabbana wa lakal hamd"

    **Translation:** "Our Lord, to You belongs all praise."

    Some add: "Hamdankathiran tayyiban mubarakan fihi" (Praise that is abundant, good, and blessed).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prostration (sujud)">
    Say "Allahu Akbar" and prostrate, placing your forehead, nose, both palms, both knees, and the toes of both feet on the ground. These seven body parts must touch the ground: the forehead (including the nose), the two palms, the two knees, and the two feet.

    Your arms should be away from your sides, and your stomach should not touch your thighs. Your fingers should face the Qibla.

    While in sujud, glorify Allah by saying three or more times:

    <p className="io-arabic">سُبْحَانَ رَبِّيَ الْأَعْلَى</p>

    **Transliteration:** "Subhana Rabbiyal A'la"

    **Translation:** "Glory be to my Lord, the Most High."

    The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "The closest a servant is to his Lord is when he is prostrating, so increase your supplications therein." (Sahih Muslim 482) You may add personal duas in any language during sujud.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sitting between prostrations (jalsah)">
    Say "Allahu Akbar" and sit up from sujud. Sit on your left foot with your right foot upright, toes facing the Qibla (this is the iftirash position). Place your hands on your thighs near the knees, fingers slightly spread.

    It is recommended to say:

    <p className="io-arabic">رَبِّ اغْفِرْ لِي</p>

    **Transliteration:** "Rabbighfir li"

    **Translation:** "My Lord, forgive me."

    Then say "Allahu Akbar" and prostrate again for the second sujud, repeating "Subhana Rabbiyal A'la" three or more times.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Standing for the second rak'ah">
    After the second sujud, say "Allahu Akbar" and stand up for the second rak'ah. Some scholars recommend sitting briefly (jalsah istirahah) before standing, especially for the elderly, but it is not obligatory.

    In the second rak'ah, repeat the steps from reciting Al-Fatihah and a short surah through to the second sujud. The only difference is that you do not need to recite the opening supplication (dua al-istiftah) again.
  </Step>
  <Step title="First sitting (tashahhud)">
    After the second sujud of the second rak'ah, instead of standing, sit for the tashahhud (testimony). Sit in the tawarruk position: sit on your left hip, place your left foot under your right thigh, and keep your right foot upright. In a two-rak'ah prayer, this is the final sitting.

    Place your right hand on your right thigh, making a fist with the little finger and ring finger, or keeping all fingers together. Point the index finger toward the Qibla and move it slightly when mentioning Allah. Some schools keep the finger still. The left hand rests on the left thigh.

    Recite the tashahhud. The version narrated from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud (may Allah be pleased with him) is:

    <p className="io-arabic">التَّحِيَّاتُ لِلَّهِ وَالصَّلَوَاتُ وَالطَّيِّبَاتُ السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكَ أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ وَرَحْمَةُ اللَّهِ وَبَرَكَاتُهُ السَّلَامُ عَلَيْنَا وَعَلَىٰ عِبَادِ اللَّهِ الصَّالِحِينَ أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُهُ وَرَسُولُهُ</p>

    **Transliteration:** "At-tahiyyatu lillahi was-salawatu wat-tayyibat. As-salamu 'alayka ayyuhan-nabiyyu wa rahmatullahi wa barakatuh. As-salamu 'alayna wa 'ala 'ibadillahis-salihin. Ashhadu an la ilaha illallahu wa ashhadu anna Muhammadan abduhu wa rasuluh."

    **Translation:** "All greetings of humility are for Allah, and all prayers and good deeds. Peace be upon you, O Prophet, and the mercy of Allah and His blessings. Peace be upon us and upon the righteous servants of Allah. I bear witness that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and messenger."
  </Step>
  <Step title="Salawat Ibrahimiyyah (blessings upon the Prophet)">
    After the tashahhud, send blessings upon the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) and his family, as Allah commands in the Quran: "Indeed, Allah confers blessing upon the Prophet, and His angels [ask Him to do so]. O you who have believed, ask [Allah to confer] blessing upon him and ask [Allah to grant him] peace." (Quran 33:56)

    The commonly recited version is:

    <p className="io-arabic">اللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَىٰ مُحَمَّدٍ وَعَلَىٰ آلِ مُحَمَّدٍ كَمَا صَلَّيْتَ عَلَىٰ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَعَلَىٰ آلِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ إِنَّكَ حَمِيدٌ مَجِيدٌ اللَّهُمَّ بَارِكْ عَلَىٰ مُحَمَّدٍ وَعَلَىٰ آلِ مُحَمَّدٍ كَمَا بَارَكْتَ عَلَىٰ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَعَلَىٰ آلِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ إِنَّكَ حَمِيدٌ مَجِيدٌ</p>

    **Transliteration:** "Allahumma salli 'ala Muhammadin wa 'ala ali Muhammadin, kama sallayta 'ala Ibrahim wa 'ala ali Ibrahim, innaka Hamidun Majid. Allahumma barik 'ala Muhammadin wa 'ala ali Muhammadin, kama barakta 'ala Ibrahim wa 'ala ali Ibrahim, innaka Hamidun Majid."

    **Translation:** "O Allah, send blessings upon Muhammad and upon the family of Muhammad, as You sent blessings upon Ibrahim and upon the family of Ibrahim. Indeed, You are Praiseworthy and Glorious. O Allah, bless Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, as You blessed Ibrahim and the family of Ibrahim. Indeed, You are Praiseworthy and Glorious."
  </Step>
  <Step title="Supplications before salam">
    It is recommended to make personal supplications after the Salawat Ibrahimiyyah and before the final salam. The Prophet (peace be upon him) taught several duas for this moment, including:

    <p className="io-arabic">اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ عَذَابِ جَهَنَّمَ وَمِنْ عَذَابِ الْقَبْرِ وَمِنْ فِتْنَةِ الْمَحْيَا وَالْمَمَاتِ وَمِنْ شَرِّ فِتْنَةِ الْمَسِيحِ الدَّجَّالِ</p>

    **Transliteration:** "Allahumma inni a'udhu bika min 'adhabi jahannam, wa min 'adhabil-qabr, wa min fitnatil-mahya wal-mamat, wa min sharri fitnatil-masihid-dajjal."

    **Translation:** "O Allah, I seek refuge in You from the punishment of Hell, from the punishment of the grave, from the trials of life and death, and from the evil of the trial of the Dajjal (False Messiah)."

    You may also say "Rabbana atina fid-dunya hasanatan wa fil-akhirati hasanatan wa qina 'adhaban-nar" (Our Lord, give us good in this world and good in the Hereafter, and protect us from the punishment of the Fire). (Quran 2:201)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turning the head for salam (taslim)">
    End the prayer by turning your head to the right and saying:

    <p className="io-arabic">السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَةُ اللَّهِ</p>

    **Transliteration:** "As-salamu 'alaykum wa rahmatullah"

    **Translation:** "Peace be upon you and the mercy of Allah."

    Then turn your head to the left and repeat the same greeting. Some scholars say one salam is sufficient, but the majority recommend two. After the salam, the prayer is complete and you are free to move and speak.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Three-rak'ah and four-rak'ah prayers

The procedure for three-rak'ah prayers (Maghrib) and four-rak'ah prayers (Dhuhr, Asr, Isha) follows the same pattern as the two-rak'ah prayer, with these differences:

### Three-rak'ah prayer (Maghrib)

1. Perform two rak'ah exactly as described above, including the first sitting (tashahhud) after the second rak'ah.
2. After the tashahhud, stand up for the third rak'ah without reciting the full tashahhud or salawat. Simply say "Allahu Akbar" and stand.
3. In the third rak'ah, recite only Surah Al-Fatihah (no additional surah).
4. Complete ruku and the two sujud as normal.
5. After the second sujud of the third rak'ah, sit for the **final tashahhud**.
6. Recite the complete tashahhud, the Salawat Ibrahimiyyah, personal duas, and then the salam to both sides.

### Four-rak'ah prayer (Dhuhr, Asr, Isha)

1. Perform two rak'ah with the first sitting (tashahhud) after the second rak'ah.
2. After the first tashahhud, stand for the third rak'ah. Recite only Al-Fatihah.
3. Complete ruku and two sujud.
4. Stand for the fourth rak'ah. Recite only Al-Fatihah.
5. Complete ruku and two sujud.
6. After the second sujud of the fourth rak'ah, sit for the **final tashahhud**.
7. Recite the complete tashahhud, the Salawat Ibrahimiyyah, personal duas, and the salam to both sides.

<Info>
  In the third and fourth rak'ah of any prayer, you recite only Surah Al-Fatihah and do not add a second surah. The first two rak'ah always include both Al-Fatihah and an additional surah or verses.
</Info>

## Friday prayer and congregational prayer

### Jumu'ah (Friday prayer)

The Friday prayer replaces Dhuhr for adult men who are resident and healthy. It consists of:

- A khutbah (sermon) in two parts, with the imam sitting briefly between them
- Two rak'ah of prayer performed in congregation
- Recited aloud, similar to Fajr

Women, children, travelers, and those with valid excuses may pray Dhuhr instead. The time for Jumu'ah is the time of Dhuhr. It is recommended to arrive early, listen attentively to the khutbah, and avoid speaking or distractions during it.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Whoever performs ghusl on Friday, purifies himself as much as he can, uses oil or perfume from his house, then goes out and does not separate two people sitting together, then prays what is prescribed for him and remains silent while the imam speaks, his sins between that Friday and the previous Friday will be forgiven." (Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim)

### Congregational prayer (salah jama'ah)

Praying in congregation is strongly emphasized for men. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Prayer in congregation is twenty-seven times better than prayer offered alone." (Sahih al-Bukhari 645, Sahih Muslim 650)

In congregation:

- The imam stands in front, facing the Qibla
- The followers stand behind the imam in straight rows
- Women stand behind the men or in a separate area
- The imam recites aloud in prayers where recitation is audible; followers listen silently
- Followers perform the same physical actions as the imam, following his movements
- When the imam says "Sami Allahu liman hamidah," the followers say "Rabbana wa lakal hamd"
- The follower should not bow or prostrate before the imam, nor delay after him

If you arrive late to congregation, join immediately and complete whatever part of the prayer you catch with the imam. After the imam finishes with salam, you make up the missed rak'ah individually.

## Things that invalidate prayer

Certain actions break the prayer and require it to be restarted. The main invalidators are:

1. **Speaking intentionally:** Any deliberate speech that is not part of the prayer invalidates it. Coughing, clearing the throat, or unintentional sounds do not invalidate the prayer.
2. **Laughing out loud:** A loud laugh that exposes the throat breaks the prayer. Smiling or a slight sound does not.
3. **Eating or drinking:** Consuming anything during prayer invalidates it.
4. **Excessive movement:** Turning the entire body away from the Qibla, or three consecutive large movements without a valid excuse, invalidates the prayer. Small movements for necessity (adjusting clothing, scratching an itch, killing a snake or scorpion) are permitted.
5. **Breaking wudu:** If you lose wudu during prayer (for example, by passing wind, urinating, or defecating), the prayer is invalidated and must be repeated after renewing wudu.
6. **Uncovering the awrah:** If a significant portion of the awrah becomes uncovered and remains uncovered for the duration of three tasbih (glorifications), the prayer is invalidated.
7. **Intentionally looking at something that distracts from prayer:** While this does not technically invalidate the prayer in most schools, it severely reduces its reward and is strongly discouraged.
8. **Leaving out an obligatory (fard) action:** Deliberately omitting a fard element such as ruku, sujud, or Al-Fatihah invalidates the prayer. Forgetting an obligatory element may be corrected with sujud as-sahw.

## Sujud as-sahw (prostration of forgetfulness)

Sujud as-sahw are two extra prostrations performed at the end of the prayer to compensate for certain mistakes or omissions made out of forgetfulness. The Prophet (peace be upon him) performed this, saying: "When one of you forgets a prayer, let him pray two prostrations of forgetfulness." (Sahih Muslim 572)

### When to perform sujud as-sahw

You should perform sujud as-sahw in the following situations:

- **Adding something:** If you accidentally add an extra rak'ah, ruku, or sujud
- **Omitting something:** If you forget an obligatory action and remember it before finishing the prayer
- **Doubt:** If you are unsure how many rak'ah you have prayed, you act upon what you are reasonably certain of and then perform sujud as-sahw
- **Missing the first sitting:** If you forget the first tashahhud after two rak'ah and stand up, you do not return; you continue and perform sujud as-sahw at the end
- **Delaying or rushing an action:** Significant delays between the pillars of prayer or performing them in the wrong order

### How to perform sujud as-sahw

Perform sujud as-sahw immediately before the final salam:

1. After completing the final tashahhud and Salawat Ibrahimiyyah, say "Allahu Akbar"
2. Prostrate once, saying "Subhana Rabbiyal A'la"
3. Sit up and say "Allahu Akbar"
4. Prostrate again, saying "Subhana Rabbiyal A'la"
5. Sit up and complete the taslim (salam) as normal

Some scholars say sujud as-sahw should be performed after the salam, but the majority opinion is to perform it before the salam. Follow the guidance of your school or local community.

<Warning>
  Sujud as-sahw only covers mistakes made out of forgetfulness. Deliberate omissions or additions require the prayer to be restarted.
</Warning>

## Tips for khushu (focus and humility)

Khushu is the state of humble submission, attentiveness, and presence of heart during prayer. It transforms Salah from a mere physical exercise into a profound spiritual experience. The Quran repeatedly commands believers to establish prayer with khushu:

<p className="io-arabic">قَدْ أَفْلَحَ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ ﴿١﴾ الَّذِينَ هُمْ فِي صَلَاتِهِمْ خَاشِعُونَ ﴿٢﴾</p>

**Translation:** "Certainly will the believers have succeeded: those who are during their prayer humbly submissive." (Quran 23:1-2)

Here are practical tips to develop khushu:

1. **Prepare before the prayer:** Perform wudu with care and presence. Pause for a moment before saying "Allahu Akbar" to collect your thoughts and remember that you are about to stand before Allah.
2. **Understand what you are saying:** Learn the meanings of the Arabic words you recite. When you understand that you are praising Allah, seeking His guidance, and acknowledging His greatness, your heart naturally engages.
3. **Fix your gaze:** Keep your eyes focused on the place of prostration during qiyam, on your feet during ruku, on your nose during sujud, and on your lap during sitting. Avoid looking around.
4. **Pray slowly and deliberately:** The Prophet (peace be upon him) prayed with measured calmness. Rushing through the positions defeats the purpose of prayer. Take time in ruku and sujud.
5. **Remove distractions:** Put your phone away or on silent. Choose a quiet place. If praying at home, inform family members that you are in prayer.
6. **Reflect on death and the Hereafter:** Remember that this prayer could be your last. The Prophet (peace be upon him) advised us to pray each prayer as if it were a farewell prayer.
7. **Vary your recitation:** Do not recite the same short surah every time. Rotate between different surahs to keep your mind engaged. Our <a href="/quran">Quran section</a> can help you learn new surahs.
8. **Use a prayer mat and face a wall:** These simple physical boundaries help reduce visual distractions.
9. **Make dua during sujud:** Since you are closest to Allah in prostration, use this time for personal supplication. Ask for what you need in any language.
10. **Pray on time:** Delaying prayer until the last moment often means praying with stress and haste. Use our <a href="/prayer-times">prayer times tool</a> to stay on schedule.

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I pray in English or another language instead of Arabic?">
    The obligatory recitations in Salah (Al-Fatihah, the additional surah, the takbirat, the tasbihat in ruku and sujud, the tashahhud, and the salam) must be in Arabic. Personal supplications (dua) during sujud, before salam, and in voluntary prayers may be in any language. If you are a new Muslim or beginner still learning Arabic, scholars permit you to say the minimum required phrases in Arabic and make personal supplications in your own language until you memorize the Arabic.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What if I forget how many rak'ah I have prayed?">
    If you are unsure, act upon what you are most certain of. For example, if you are unsure whether you prayed two or three rak'ah, assume the lower number and complete the prayer from there. Then perform sujud as-sahw before the salam. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "If one of you doubts in his prayer, let him leave the doubt and act upon what he is certain of." (Sahih Muslim 571)
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do women pray differently from men?">
    The core structure of the prayer is the same for men and women. There are minor differences in recommended posture: women keep their bodies more compact, arms closer to the sides, and may sit in a slightly different position. These differences are based on the authentic narrations from the female Companions. The essential pillars (arkaan) and obligatory actions (wajibaat) are identical.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I pray sitting down or lying down if I am sick or unable to stand?">
    Yes. Allah does not burden a soul beyond what it can bear. If you are unable to stand, you may pray sitting. If you cannot sit, you may pray lying on your side or back, gesturing for ruku and sujud (by lowering your head if possible). If you cannot move your head, you may gesture with your eyes. If you cannot gesture at all, you pray with your heart. The reward is based on your intention and effort, not the physical perfection of the movements.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What should I do if I pass wind during prayer?">
    Passing wind breaks wudu, which breaks the prayer. You should leave the prayer immediately, renew your wudu, and restart the prayer from the beginning. Do not try to hold it or continue, as the prayer is already invalid.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is it okay to pray in clothes that have a small spot of impurity?">
    A small spot of impurity that is less than the size of a dirham (approximately the size of a US quarter or the inner circle of your palm) is excused according to the majority of scholars, especially if removing it would cause hardship. However, it is always better and more cautious to wear completely clean clothes for prayer.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I make up (qada) prayers I missed in the past?">
    Missed obligatory prayers must be made up. There is scholarly consensus that deliberately missed prayers are a serious sin and must be compensated. The method is to estimate how many prayers you missed and make them up one by one, praying them in order (Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, Isha) for each day missed. Some scholars allow making them up in any order if remembering the exact order is difficult. You can pray qada after each current prayer or dedicate specific times for making up missed prayers. Do not delay; start today.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is the difference between fard, wajib, sunnah, and nafl prayers?">
    **Fard** (obligatory) prayers are the five daily prayers. Missing them without a valid excuse is a major sin. **Wajib** (necessary) prayers are slightly below fard in legal weight, such as Witr and Eid prayers. Missing them is sinful but less severe than missing fard. **Sunnah** prayers are those the Prophet (peace be upon him) regularly performed, such as the rawatib before and after fard prayers. Abandoning them is disliked but not sinful. **Nafl** (voluntary) prayers are extra prayers performed for additional reward, such as tahajjud, duha, and voluntary prayers at any time. They are highly recommended and bring great reward.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Conclusion

Salah is the foundation of a Muslim's daily life. It is a gift from Allah, a direct line of communication, and a source of peace, guidance, and spiritual nourishment. Learning to pray correctly may feel overwhelming at first, but every step you take is rewarded, and every prayer you perform brings you closer to Allah.

Start with the basics: make wudu properly, learn Al-Fatihah and a few short surahs, and follow the steps in this guide. Over time, the movements will become natural, and your heart will find the tranquility that comes from standing before your Creator five times each day.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "The first matter that the slave will be brought to account for on the Day of Judgment is the prayer. If it is sound, then the rest of his deeds will be sound. And if it is corrupt, then the rest of his deeds will be corrupt." (At-Tabarani; authenticated by some scholars)

May Allah accept your prayers, deepen your khushu, and make Salah the coolness of your eyes and the light of your heart. Ameen.
