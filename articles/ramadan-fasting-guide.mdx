Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic lunar calendar and the holiest period in the Muslim year. It is the month in which the Quran was revealed, a month of fasting, prayer, reflection, and community. Allah says in the Quran:

<p className="io-arabic">يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الصِّيَامُ كَمَا كُتِبَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ</p>

And:

<p className="io-arabic">شَهْرُ رَمَضَانَ الَّذِي أُنزِلَ فِيهِ الْقُرْآنُ هُدًى لِّلنَّاسِ وَبَيِّنَاتٍ مِّنَ الْهُدَىٰ وَالْفُرْقَانِ</p>

"The month of Ramadan is that in which the Quran was revealed as guidance for the people and clear proofs of guidance and criterion." (Quran 2:185)

Whether you are new to Islam, a revert, or a born Muslim seeking a refresher, this page is designed to help you make the most of this blessed month.

## What Is Ramadan?

Ramadan is the ninth month of the [Islamic (Hijri) calendar](/islamic-calendar), a lunar calendar consisting of twelve months of 29 or 30 days each. Because the Islamic year is roughly 354 days long, Ramadan shifts backward through the solar year by about 10 to 12 days annually. This means Muslims around the world experience Ramadan in different seasons over the course of their lives, from long summer days to shorter winter days.

The word "Ramadan" comes from the Arabic root _ramida_ or _ar-ramad_, meaning scorching heat or dryness. Scholars say this reflects the burning away of sins through fasting and devotion. The month is one of the Five Pillars of Islam, the foundational acts of worship that define a Muslim's faith and practice. You can read more about the pillars in our article on the [Five Pillars of Islam](/articles/five-pillars-of-islam).

### The Revelation of the Quran

Ramadan holds special significance because it is the month in which the Quran was first revealed. The Night of Decree (Laylat al-Qadr), which falls within the last ten nights of Ramadan, is described in the Quran as better than a thousand months:

<p className="io-arabic">لَيْلَةُ الْقَدْرِ خَيْرٌ مِّنْ أَلْفِ شَهْرٍ</p>

"The Night of Decree is better than a thousand months." (Quran 97:3)

Muslims therefore increase their recitation of the Quran during Ramadan, with many completing one full reading (khatm) during the month. The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) would review the Quran with the Angel Jibreel (Gabriel) every Ramadan.

### The Gates of Heaven and Hell

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said that when Ramadan begins, the gates of Paradise are opened, the gates of Hell are closed, and the devils are chained. This does not mean evil ceases entirely, but it means the avenues of temptation are reduced and the opportunities for good are multiplied. It is a time when good deeds are magnified, and Muslims are encouraged to seize the moment.

## Who Must Fast and Who Is Exempt

Fasting during Ramadan is obligatory (fard) for every adult Muslim who is physically and mentally able to do so. The conditions for the obligation are:

- **Muslim**: Fasting is an act of Islamic worship and is not required of non-Muslims.
- **Puberty**: Children are not required to fast, though many begin partial fasting to build the habit.
- **Sanity**: A person must be of sound mind.
- **Physical ability**: The person must be healthy enough to fast without harm.
- **Residence**: Travellers have a concession (see below).
- **Not menstruating or in postnatal bleeding**: Women in these states do not fast but must make up the days later.

### Categories of Exemption

Allah mentions exemptions clearly in the Quran:

<p className="io-arabic">فَمَن كَانَ مِنكُم مَّرِيضًا أَوْ عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍ فَعِدَّةٌ مِّنْ أَيَّامٍ أُخَرَ</p>

"So whoever among you is ill or on a journey, then an equal number of days are to be made up." (Quran 2:184)

The main categories of exemption are:

| Category | Ruling | Details |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Illness | Make up later | Temporary illness that would worsen with fasting |
| Chronic illness (elderly, frail) | Fidyah | Feeding one poor person for each day missed; scholarly opinions differ on whether making up is also required if recovery is possible |
| Travel | Make up later | Significant travel (roughly 80 km or more); some scholars allow breaking the fast for any genuinely difficult journey |
| Pregnancy and nursing | Make up later | If fasting would harm the mother or child; scholarly opinions differ on whether fidyah is also due |
| Menstruation and postnatal bleeding | Make up later | Must abstain from fasting during these days and make them up after Ramadan |
| Intense thirst or hunger | Make up later | If there is genuine danger to health; this is a concession, not a license to break the fast lightly |

### Making Up Missed Fasts (Qada)

Those who miss fasts due to temporary illness, travel, menstruation, or other valid reasons must make up the same number of days after Ramadan, before the next Ramadan begins. The exact timing is flexible, though sooner is better.

### Fidyah: Feeding the Poor

For those who cannot fast and cannot reasonably expect to make up the fasts (such as the elderly with chronic conditions), the Quran mentions an alternative:

<p className="io-arabic">وَعَلَى الَّذِينَ يُطِيقُونَهُ فِدْيَةٌ طَعَامُ مِسْكِينٍ</p>

"And upon those who are able to fast with difficulty, a ransom of feeding a poor person." (Quran 2:184)

The standard fidyah is to feed one poor person for each day missed. This can be done by providing meals or by giving the equivalent monetary value to a trusted charitable organization. Scholars differ on whether fidyah replaces the fast entirely or is in addition to making up the fast if recovery becomes possible. When in doubt, consult a knowledgeable local scholar.

## The Intention (Niyyah)

The intention is an essential condition for the validity of the fast. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Actions are but by intentions, and every person will have what they intended." (Sahih al-Bukhari 1, Sahih Muslim 1907)

For Ramadan fasting, the intention must be made each night before dawn (fajr). It does not need to be spoken aloud; a silent resolve in the heart is sufficient. The intention distinguishes the fast from mere abstinence from food. If someone eats before dawn without intending to fast, and then decides during the day to fast, the fast is not valid for Ramadan (though it may count as a voluntary fast if the intention is made before noon, according to some scholars).

A helpful practice is to make the intention part of the pre-dawn routine: before eating suhur, pause and form the intention in the heart to fast the following day for the sake of Allah.

## Suhur: The Pre-Dawn Meal

Suhur is the pre-dawn meal eaten before the fajr prayer and before the fast begins. It is a strongly recommended sunnah, not obligatory, but the Prophet (peace be upon him) encouraged it greatly:

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Take suhur, for in suhur there is blessing." (Sahih al-Bukhari 1923, Sahih Muslim 1095)

Another narration states: "The difference between our fasting and the fasting of the People of the Book is the meal of suhur." (Sahih Muslim 1096)

### Timing of Suhur

Suhur should be eaten close to dawn, but before the adhan of fajr. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "My nation will continue to be upon goodness as long as they hasten to break the fast and delay suhur." (Sahih al-Bukhari 1957, Sahih Muslim 1098)

The exact time for stopping eating is when the first light of dawn appears on the horizon (fajr al-sadiq, the true dawn), not the earlier astronomical twilight. Many Muslims use [prayer time calculators](/prayer-times) or local mosque schedules to determine the precise cutoff time in their location.

### What to Eat for Suhur

The best suhur is filling, nutritious, and slow-digesting. Recommended foods include:

- **Complex carbohydrates**: Oats, whole grain bread, brown rice (provide sustained energy)
- **Protein**: Eggs, yogurt, beans, lentils (reduce hunger)
- **Healthy fats**: Nuts, avocado, olive oil (provide satiety)
- **Fiber-rich foods**: Vegetables, fruits with skin, legumes (aid digestion)
- **Hydrating foods**: Watermelon, cucumber, soups (help with hydration)

Avoid excessively salty or sugary foods, which can increase thirst and cause energy crashes later in the day.

## Iftar: Breaking the Fast

Iftar is the meal with which Muslims break their fast at sunset (maghrib). It is a moment of joy, gratitude, and community. The Prophet (peace be upon him) encouraged hastening to break the fast:

"The people will continue to be upon goodness as long as they hasten to break the fast." (Sahih al-Bukhari 1957, Sahih Muslim 1098)

The fast is broken at sunset, when the sun has completely disappeared below the horizon. It is recommended to break the fast with dates and water, following the practice of the Prophet (peace be upon him):

"When one of you breaks his fast, let him break it with dates, for that is a blessing. If he cannot find dates, then water, for it is pure." (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 694, graded hasan)

### Dua for Breaking the Fast

It is recommended to recite the following supplication when breaking the fast:

<p className="io-arabic">ذَهَبَ الظَّمَأُ وَابْتَلَّتِ الْعُرُوقُ وَثَبَتَ الْأَجْرُ إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ</p>

"The thirst is gone, the veins are moistened, and the reward is confirmed, if Allah wills." (Sunan Abu Dawud 2357, graded hasan)

Another commonly recited supplication is:

<p className="io-arabic">اللَّهُمَّ لَكَ صُمْتُ وَعَلَى رِزْقِكَ أَفْطَرْتُ</p>

"O Allah, for You I have fasted, and with Your provision I break my fast." (Sunan Abu Dawud, graded da'if by some scholars, but widely practiced)

### Iftar Etiquette

- Break the fast immediately at sunset; do not delay unnecessarily
- Begin with dates and water, then pray maghrib if it is time
- Eat in moderation; the stomach has shrunk during the day
- Share the meal with family, neighbors, and those in need
- Avoid wastefulness and excessive indulgence

Many mosques and communities host communal iftars, which strengthen social bonds and provide meals to those who might otherwise fast alone.

## What Breaks the Fast and What Does Not

Understanding what invalidates the fast is essential for observing it correctly. The fast is broken by anything that enters the body through a normal pathway (the mouth, nose, or other orifices) in a deliberate manner, as well as by certain other acts.

### Actions That Break the Fast

The following require both making up the fast (qada) and may require expiation (kaffarah) if done deliberately without a valid excuse:

| Action | Ruling | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eating or drinking deliberately | Breaks fast | Includes food, drink, and smoking |
| Vomiting deliberately | Breaks fast | Deliberately inducing vomit invalidates the fast; involuntary vomiting does not |
| Sexual intercourse | Breaks fast + kaffarah | Requires making up the fast and freeing a slave, or fasting two consecutive months, or feeding sixty poor people (if unable to fast) |
| Menstruation or postnatal bleeding | Breaks fast | The fast is invalidated automatically; days must be made up |
| Ejaculation from deliberate stimulation | Breaks fast | Wet dreams do not break the fast |

### Actions That Do Not Break the Fast

The following do not invalidate the fast:

- **Involuntary actions**: Eating or drinking out of forgetfulness, being forced, or making a mistake
- **Unintentional vomiting**: Vomiting that occurs without deliberate inducement
- **Wet dreams**: Nocturnal emission during sleep
- **Rinsing the mouth and nose**: During wudu, provided water is not swallowed excessively
- **Applying eye drops, ear drops, or skin creams**: These do not reach the stomach in a nutritive way
- **Injections that are not nutritive**: Medical injections (vaccines, insulin, non-nutritive medication) do not break the fast according to the majority of scholars; nutritive injections do
- **Tasting food without swallowing**: For cooking purposes, though it is better to avoid
- **Using the miswak or toothbrush**: Without toothpaste that is swallowed

### The Forgetfulness Exception

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "If somebody eats or drinks forgetfully, then he should complete his fast, for what he has eaten or drunk has been given to him by Allah." (Sahih al-Bukhari 1933, Sahih Muslim 1155)

This is a beautiful mercy: if someone genuinely forgets they are fasting and eats or drinks, they should stop as soon as they remember, and their fast remains valid. They do not need to make it up.

### Swallowing Water During Wudu

Care should be taken during wudu not to swallow water intentionally. Rinsing the mouth is part of wudu, but excessive gargling or swallowing water invalidates the fast. Similarly, water entering the nose should not be snuffed deeply to avoid it reaching the throat.

## Taraweeh and Qiyam al-Layl

Taraweeh are the voluntary night prayers performed during Ramadan after the isha prayer. The word means "to rest and relax," because the prayer involves brief pauses after every four rak'ahs. These prayers are a distinctive feature of Ramadan and a means of earning immense reward.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) prayed qiyam (night prayer) in congregation for a few nights, then stopped out of fear that it would become obligatory. The caliph Umar later organized the congregation again, saying: "What an excellent innovation this is," meaning the revival of a sunnah that had been left. (Sahih al-Bukhari 2010, Sahih Muslim 761)

### How Taraweeh Is Performed

Taraweeh consists of sets of two rak'ahs each. The most common practices are:

- **8 rak'ahs**: Following the practice of the Prophet (peace be upon him), who never exceeded eleven rak'ahs in any night prayer
- **20 rak'ahs**: The practice established by Caliph Umar and followed by the majority of mosques worldwide, particularly in the Hanafi and Shafi'i schools

Scholars differ on the optimal number, and both are valid. What matters is praying with sincerity and concentration. Many mosques complete one full Quran recitation (khatm) over the course of Ramadan during taraweeh.

### Qiyam al-Layl (Night Vigil)

Qiyam al-Layl refers to any night prayer, including tahajjud. During Ramadan, the last third of the night is especially blessed. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Our Lord descends every night to the lowest heaven when the last third of the night remains, and He says: 'Who is calling upon Me that I may answer him? Who is asking of Me that I may give him? Who is seeking My forgiveness that I may forgive him?'" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1145, Sahih Muslim 758)

Muslims are encouraged to wake for qiyam, especially in the last ten nights of Ramadan, to seek Laylat al-Qadr and the forgiveness it brings.

## Laylat al-Qadr: The Night of Decree

Laylat al-Qadr, the Night of Decree (or Night of Power), is the holiest night of the year. It falls within the last ten nights of Ramadan, most likely on one of the odd nights: the 21st, 23rd, 25th, 27th, or 29th. The majority of scholars favor the 27th night, but because the exact date is concealed, Muslims strive on all of the last ten nights.

Allah says:

<p className="io-arabic">إِنَّا أَنزَلْنَاهُ فِي لَيْلَةِ الْقَدْرِ وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا لَيْلَةُ الْقَدْرِ لَيْلَةُ الْقَدْرِ خَيْرٌ مِّنْ أَلْفِ شَهْرٍ</p>

"Indeed, We sent it down during the Night of Decree. And what can make you know what the Night of Decree is? The Night of Decree is better than a thousand months." (Quran 97:1-3)

### Signs of Laylat al-Qadr

The Prophet (peace be upon him) described signs that may indicate the night:

- It is a pleasant night, neither hot nor cold
- The sun rises the next morning without strong rays, appearing weak and reddish
- The night is calm and peaceful

### What to Do on Laylat al-Qadr

Muslims should maximize worship during the last ten nights, and especially on Laylat al-Qadr:

- **Pray qiyam al-layl**: Spend the night in prayer and Quran recitation
- **Make dua**: The Prophet (peace be upon him) taught a specific supplication:

<p className="io-arabic">اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّكَ عَفُوٌّ تُحِبُّ الْعَفْوَ فَاعْفُ عَنِّي</p>

"O Allah, You are pardoning and You love to pardon, so pardon me." (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 3513, graded hasan)

- **Give charity**: Good deeds on this night are multiplied beyond measure
- **Seek forgiveness**: Ask Allah for forgiveness for past sins
- **Make dua for others**: Include family, the deceased, the ummah, and all of humanity

Missing Laylat al-Qadr is considered a great loss, which is why the Prophet (peace be upon him) would tighten his belt (metaphorically, increase his efforts) during the last ten nights and wake his family for worship.

## I'tikaf: Spiritual Retreat

I'tikaf is the spiritual retreat in the mosque during the last ten days of Ramadan, seeking Laylat al-Qadr and devoting oneself entirely to worship. The Prophet (peace be upon him) performed i'tikaf regularly in the last ten days of Ramadan, and he did so in the year of his death for twenty days.

### Conditions of I'tikaf

- **Intention**: Must be made with sincerity for the sake of Allah
- **Location**: Must be performed in a mosque (for men; women may perform it in their designated prayer area at home according to some scholars)
- **Duration**: Minimum of one day and night for a complete i'tikaf; the sunnah is the last ten days of Ramadan
- **Abstention from sin**: The person in i'tikaf must avoid all sinful speech and actions
- **Permissible activities**: Prayer, Quran recitation, dhikr, dua, Islamic study, eating, drinking, sleeping, and necessary bodily functions

### What Invalidates I'tikaf

Leaving the mosque without a valid reason (such as using the restroom or attending Friday prayer if the mosque has no facilities) invalidates i'tikaf. Intimate relations, menstruation, and deliberate major sins also break the retreat.

Many mosques organize communal i'tikaf programs, providing meals and facilities for those who wish to spend the last ten nights in devotion.

## Zakat al-Fitr

Zakat al-Fitr (also called Sadaqat al-Fitr) is a charitable donation given before the Eid prayer at the end of Ramadan. It is obligatory upon every Muslim who possesses food in excess of their needs for the day and night of Eid, including dependents such as children and elderly parents.

### Purpose of Zakat al-Fitr

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Zakat al-Fitr is purification for the fasting person from idle talk and obscenity, and it is food for the poor." (Sunan Abu Dawud 1609, graded hasan)

It serves two purposes:

1. To purify the fasting person from any shortcomings in their fast
2. To ensure that the poor and needy can also enjoy Eid without worry

### Amount and Timing

The amount is one sa' of the staple food of the locality, which is approximately 2.5 to 3 kilograms of wheat, barley, dates, raisins, rice, or similar. Many Muslims give the monetary equivalent instead, which is often more practical and beneficial. The amount varies by region and food prices; local mosques and Islamic centers typically announce the recommended amount each year.

Zakat al-Fitr must be given before the Eid prayer. If given after the prayer, it is considered ordinary charity (sadaqah) and does not fulfill the obligation of Zakat al-Fitr. It is permissible to give it a day or two before Eid.

You can learn more about zakat calculations in our [Zakat Complete Guide](/articles/zakat-complete-guide) and use our [Zakat Calculator](/tools/zakat-calculator) to help determine your obligations.

## Eid al-Fitr

Eid al-Fitr, the Festival of Breaking the Fast, marks the end of Ramadan and the beginning of Shawwal, the tenth month of the Islamic calendar. It is a day of joy, gratitude, celebration, and community.

### The Eid Prayer

The Eid prayer is a communal obligation (fard kifayah) performed in congregation, usually in open spaces or large mosques. It consists of two rak'ahs with additional takbeers (saying "Allahu Akbar") in each. The sermon (khutbah) follows the prayer, unlike the Friday prayer where the sermon precedes it.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) would eat an odd number of dates before leaving for the Eid prayer, to signify that the fast was over.

### Eid Etiquette and Practices

- **Ghusl (ritual bath)**: It is sunnah to perform ghusl before the Eid prayer
- **Best clothes**: Wearing clean, festive clothing, preferably new if possible
- **Takbeer**: Reciting the Eid takbeer from sunset on the night before Eid until the imam begins the prayer:

<p className="io-arabic">اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ وَلِلَّهِ الْحَمْدُ</p>

- **Visiting family and friends**: Strengthening ties and spreading joy
- **Giving gifts**: Especially to children
- **Visiting the graves**: Remembering deceased loved ones and praying for them
- **Charity**: Continuing to give, as the need does not end with Ramadan

### The Six Fasts of Shawwal

After Ramadan, it is highly recommended to fast six days in Shawwal. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Whoever fasts Ramadan and then follows it with six days of Shawwal, it is as if they fasted the entire year." (Sahih Muslim 1164)

These can be fasted consecutively or separately, at any time during Shawwal except on Eid day itself. They are voluntary and bring multiplied reward.

## Sample Daily Routine

A well-structured day helps maximize the spiritual and physical benefits of Ramadan. Below is a sample routine that can be adapted to your local prayer times and personal circumstances. Use our [Prayer Times](/prayer-times) tool to find accurate timings for your location.

| Time | Activity | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4:00 AM | Wake up for suhur | Adjust based on local fajr time |
| 4:15 AM | Suhur meal | Eat slowly, drink water, make intention |
| 4:30 AM | Fajr prayer | Pray at the beginning of its time |
| 4:45 AM | Quran recitation / Dhikr | The morning hours are blessed for worship |
| 6:00 AM | Sleep or begin daily tasks | Depending on work/school schedule |
| 12:00 PM | Dhuhr prayer | Take a brief break for prayer |
| 2:00 PM | Light work / study | Energy may dip; plan less demanding tasks |
| 3:30 PM | Asr prayer | Mid-afternoon spiritual pause |
| 5:00 PM | Quran reading / Dua | Prepare spiritually for iftar |
| 6:30 PM | Prepare for iftar | Set the table, help family |
| 7:15 PM | Break fast (Iftar) | Begin with dates and water, then pray maghrib |
| 7:45 PM | Maghrib prayer | Pray in congregation if possible |
| 8:00 PM | Iftar meal | Eat moderately, include vegetables and protein |
| 9:00 PM | Rest / family time | Visit relatives, call distant family |
| 10:00 PM | Isha prayer | The night prayer begins |
| 10:30 PM | Taraweeh prayer | At home or in the mosque |
| 11:30 PM | Quran recitation / Dua | Continue worship or rest before qiyam |
| 2:00 AM | Tahajjud / Qiyam al-layl | Especially in the last ten nights |
| 3:00 AM | Sleep until suhur | Rest before the next day begins |

This schedule is a template. Those with work or school commitments should adapt it, prioritizing the obligatory prayers and fitting in taraweeh and Quran recitation where possible. The key is consistency, not perfection.

## Health and Nutrition Tips

Fasting for 14 to 18 hours (or longer in high-latitude summers) places unique demands on the body. With mindful nutrition and self-care, Muslims can maintain energy, focus, and health throughout the month.

<Note>
  The following tips are general guidance. If you have a medical condition, are pregnant, nursing, or taking medication, consult your doctor before and during Ramadan. Do not stop prescribed medication without medical advice.
</Note>

### Hydration

Dehydration is the most common challenge during fasting. To stay hydrated:

- Drink at least 8 to 10 glasses of water between iftar and suhur
- Avoid caffeinated drinks (coffee, tea, soda) which increase urination
- Include hydrating foods like watermelon, cucumber, soups, and herbal teas
- Limit salty foods that increase thirst
- Drink water steadily between iftar and bedtime, rather than all at once

### Balanced Iftar

After a day of fasting, the temptation is to overeat. Instead:

- Break the fast with dates and water, then pause before the main meal
- Include a variety of food groups: vegetables, lean protein, whole grains, and healthy fats
- Avoid deep-fried foods, excessive sweets, and heavy creamy dishes
- Eat slowly to allow the body to register fullness
- Stop eating before feeling completely full

### Suhur Strategy

- Choose slow-digesting foods that release energy gradually
- Include protein (eggs, yogurt, beans) to reduce hunger
- Avoid simple sugars that cause a crash mid-morning
- Set multiple alarms so you do not miss suhur

### Sleep and Rest

- Aim for 7 to 8 hours of total sleep, even if split between night and day
- Take a short nap after dhuhr if possible (qaylulah, midday rest)
- Avoid excessive screen time before sleep
- Create a cool, dark sleeping environment

### Exercise

- Light exercise (walking, stretching) is beneficial, especially before iftar
- Avoid intense workouts during the fast, especially in hot weather
- Schedule strength training or cardio after iftar, when energy is restored
- Listen to your body: dizziness, extreme fatigue, or chest pain are warning signs

### Managing Medical Conditions

- **Diabetes**: Work with your doctor to adjust medication timing and monitor blood sugar. Some diabetics may be exempt from fasting if it poses a risk.
- **Hypertension**: Many blood pressure medications can be taken at iftar or suhur; consult your physician.
- **Migraines**: Dehydration and caffeine withdrawal can trigger migraines. Gradually reduce caffeine before Ramadan and maintain hydration.
- **Pregnancy and nursing**: The majority of scholars exempt pregnant and nursing women if fasting would harm them or the child. Make up the fasts later or pay fidyah, depending on your situation and scholarly guidance.

## Spiritual Goals for Ramadan

Ramadan is not merely about abstaining from food and drink. It is a comprehensive spiritual training program designed to transform the soul. Setting clear spiritual goals helps make the most of this limited time.

### Quran Goals

- Complete one full reading (khatm) of the Quran during Ramadan
- Read with understanding: use a translation or tafsir alongside the Arabic
- Memorize a new surah or set of verses
- Listen to Quran recitation during commutes or chores

### Prayer Goals

- Pray all five daily prayers on time and in congregation where possible
- Never miss taraweeh; attend the mosque for the communal experience
- Add voluntary prayers (nawafil) before and after the obligatory prayers
- Wake for tahajjud at least once a week, and nightly in the last ten nights

### Character and Behavior

- Guard the tongue: avoid gossip, backbiting, arguing, and foul language
- Control anger and respond with patience
- Forgive those who have wronged you
- Increase in gratitude and positive speech

### Charity and Service

- Give daily charity, even if small
- Volunteer at a food bank, mosque, or community organization
- Help neighbors, especially the elderly and those fasting alone
- Sponsor an iftar for those in need

### Dua and Dhikr

- Make a list of specific duas for yourself, family, and the ummah
- Learn the duas for morning and evening, entering and leaving the home, and before sleeping
- Increase in saying SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah, Allahu Akbar, and La ilaha illallah
- Use the [Duas](/dua) section of this site to find authentic supplications

### Digital Detox

- Reduce time on social media and entertainment
- Replace scrolling with Quran reading or Islamic podcasts
- Use apps to track prayer times and Quran progress
- Avoid content that distracts from the spiritual focus of the month

## Frequently Asked Questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I brush my teeth while fasting?">
    Yes, you can brush your teeth with a miswak or toothbrush during the fast, provided you do not swallow toothpaste or water. It is best to use a small amount of toothpaste and rinse carefully without swallowing. Some scholars prefer using a miswak (tooth-stick) as it was the practice of the Prophet (peace be upon him) and does not involve water.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does using an asthma inhaler break the fast?">
    The majority of scholars hold that using an asthma inhaler does not break the fast, because the medication is inhaled into the lungs and does not reach the stomach in a nutritive manner. However, there is a minority view that considers it to break the fast. If you have severe asthma, your health takes priority, and you may fast on days you do not need the inhaler or make up the fasts later. Consult your doctor and a knowledgeable scholar.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What should I do if I accidentally eat or drink?">
    If you genuinely forget you are fasting and eat or drink, your fast remains valid. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "If somebody eats or drinks forgetfully, then he should complete his fast, for what he has eaten or drunk has been given to him by Allah." (Sahih al-Bukhari 1933, Sahih Muslim 1155) Stop immediately when you remember, and continue the fast normally.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I fast while traveling?">
    Travelers are permitted to break their fast, but they may choose to fast if the journey is not overly difficult. The Quran says: "So whoever among you is ill or on a journey, then an equal number of days are to be made up." (2:184) If you break the fast while traveling, you must make up the missed days later. If you fast while traveling, it counts as a valid Ramadan fast.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do children have to fast?">
    Children who have not reached puberty are not required to fast. However, many parents encourage children to practice with half-day fasts or a few days during Ramadan to build the habit and appreciate the month. The emphasis should be on positive encouragement, not force. When a child reaches puberty, fasting becomes obligatory.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is the ruling on eye drops, ear drops, and injections while fasting?">
    Eye drops and ear drops do not break the fast according to the majority of scholars, as they do not reach the stomach through a normal pathway. Non-nutritive injections (such as vaccines, insulin, or most medications) also do not break the fast. Nutritive injections (such as glucose or IV feeding) do break the fast. When in doubt, consult a knowledgeable scholar.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I listen to music during Ramadan?">
    The use of musical instruments is a matter of scholarly difference, with the majority of classical scholars considering it prohibited. During Ramadan, it is especially recommended to avoid anything that distracts from worship and spiritual focus. Many Muslims replace music with Quran recitation, nasheeds (vocal-only Islamic songs), or silence during this month.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What if I cannot make up my missed fasts before the next Ramadan?">
    If you have a valid excuse (such as a long-term illness or continuous pregnancy and nursing across multiple Ramadans) and cannot make up the fasts, you should pay fidyah (feeding one poor person for each day missed) according to the majority of scholars. If you miss fasts without a valid excuse and fail to make them up before the next Ramadan, some scholars say you must make them up and also pay fidyah. Consult a local scholar for guidance on your specific situation.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Conclusion

Ramadan is a gift from Allah, a month in which the believer draws closer to their Creator through fasting, prayer, charity, and self-discipline. It is a time when the Quran was revealed, when the gates of mercy are opened, and when a single night can be worth more than a lifetime of worship.

The fast of Ramadan is not merely the abstention from food and drink. It is a shield against sin, a training ground for patience, and a reminder of the hunger and suffering of millions around the world. It teaches gratitude for blessings we take for granted and humility before the One who provides them.

As you prepare for or continue your Ramadan journey, remember that perfection is not the goal. Consistency is. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "The most beloved of deeds to Allah are those that are most consistent, even if they are small." (Sahih al-Bukhari 6464, Sahih Muslim 2818)

May Allah accept your fasts, your prayers, your charity, and your striving. May He grant you Laylat al-Qadr and the forgiveness it brings. May this Ramadan be a turning point in your life, a month after which you emerge stronger in faith, clearer in purpose, and closer to your Lord.
