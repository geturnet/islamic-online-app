Zakat is the third pillar of Islam, a compulsory act of worship that purifies wealth and uplifts the community. Every Muslim who meets the conditions is obligated to give a portion of their surplus wealth to those in need. This guide explains the meaning of zakat, who must pay it, how to calculate it, what assets are subject to it, and where it should go.

Allah commands believers in the Quran:

<p className="io-arabic">خُذْ مِنْ أَمْوَالِهِمْ صَدَقَةً تُطَهِّرُهُمْ وَتُزَكِّيهِم بِهَا وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ ۖ إِنَّ صَلَاتَكَ سَكَنٌ لَّهُمْ ۗ وَاللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ</p>

"Take, (O Muhammad), from their wealth a charity by which you purify them and cause them increase, and invoke (Allah's blessings) upon them. Indeed, your invocations are reassurance for them. And Allah is Hearing and Knowing." (Quran 9:103)

And Allah says:

<p className="io-arabic">وَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ وَارْكَعُوا مَعَ الرَّاكِعِينَ</p>

"And establish prayer and give zakat and bow with those who bow (in worship and obedience)." (Quran 2:43)

These verses establish zakat as a fundamental duty alongside prayer. The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) emphasized its importance so strongly that he likened the denial of zakat to apostasy, and instructed that zakat be collected by the state and distributed to its rightful recipients.

## The Linguistic and Spiritual Meaning of Zakat

The Arabic word "zakat" (زكاة) comes from the root z-k-w (ز ك و), which carries meanings of purification, growth, and increase. In Islamic usage, it refers to the obligatory charitable payment that purifies the giver's wealth and soul while bringing blessings and increase to what remains.

The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) said:

"Wealth is not diminished by giving charity." (Sahih Muslim 2588)

This hadith captures the spiritual paradox of zakat: by giving away a portion of wealth, the believer does not lose. Rather, the wealth is purified from greed and attachment, and what remains grows in barakah (blessing). The word "zakat" itself implies this dual meaning: purification and growth.

Zakat also purifies the heart of the giver from miserliness and selfishness. It cultivates empathy for the poor, gratitude for God's provision, and detachment from materialism. In this way, zakat is both a financial transaction and a spiritual discipline.

<Info>
  Zakat is sometimes called "zakat al-mal" (zakat on wealth) to distinguish it from zakat al-fitr, the charity given at the end of Ramadan. Both are obligatory, but they have different rules, timing, and purposes.
</Info>

## Who Must Pay Zakat: The Conditions

For zakat to be obligatory on a person, the following conditions must be met. Scholars of the major schools of Islamic law (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali) generally agree on these conditions, though there are some differences in detail.

### 1. Islam

Zakat is only obligatory upon Muslims. A non-Muslim is not required to pay zakat, though they may give voluntary charity (sadaqah).

### 2. Sanity and Majority (Buligh)

The majority of scholars hold that zakat is obligatory only on adults who are of sound mind. A child or an insane person is not required to pay zakat on their own wealth.

<Note>
  The Hanafi school differs on this point. According to the Hanafi madhhab, zakat is due on the wealth of a child or an insane person if it reaches nisab and a hawl passes, because zakat is a right of the poor attached to the wealth itself. The guardian is responsible for paying it from the person's assets. Other schools hold that the obligation begins only when the person reaches adulthood and sanity.
</Note>

### 3. Full Ownership and Control

The wealth must be fully owned and under the person's control. Wealth that is borrowed, disputed, or inaccessible does not require zakat. The owner must have the legal and practical ability to dispose of the wealth.

### 4. Reaching the Nisab Threshold

The total zakatable wealth must equal or exceed the nisab, the minimum amount set by Islamic law. If wealth falls below nisab at any point during the lunar year, the hawl (yearly cycle) is interrupted and begins again when nisab is reached.

### 5. Completion of One Lunar Year (Hawl)

A full lunar year (hawl) must pass while the wealth remains at or above the nisab threshold. The Islamic lunar year is approximately 354 days, about 11 days shorter than the solar year.

<Note>
  Some scholars, particularly in the Hanafi school, hold that certain types of wealth (such as trade goods and money from the sale of harvest) are zakatable immediately upon receipt without waiting for a full hawl, provided the person already has other zakatable wealth that has completed a hawl. This is a matter of scholarly difference, and Muslims should consult knowledgeable scholars or follow their madhhab.
</Note>

### Summary of Conditions

| Condition | Majority View | Hanafi View (where different) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Islam | Required | Same |
| Sanity | Required for obligation | Zakat due on wealth of insane person |
| Adulthood | Required for obligation | Zakat due on wealth of child |
| Full ownership | Required | Same |
| Nisab reached | Required | Same |
| Full lunar year passed | Required | Same; some assets immediate |

## Nisab: The Minimum Threshold

Nisab is the minimum amount of wealth a Muslim must possess before zakat becomes obligatory. It is based on the value of specific quantities of gold and silver established at the time of the Prophet (peace be upon him).

### The Classical Measurements

The nisab for gold is **20 mithqal** of pure gold. The nisab for silver is **200 dirhams** of silver.

When converted to modern metric measurements:

- **Gold nisab**: approximately **85 grams** of pure gold (24 karat)
- **Silver nisab**: approximately **595 grams** of pure silver

<Note>
  Some sources give slightly different gram figures for nisab (for example, 87.48 grams for gold or 612.36 grams for silver) due to variations in how the classical weight of a mithqal or dirham is calculated. These small differences arise from scholarly debate about the exact weight of the Prophet's era coins. The figures of 85g and 595g are widely used and accepted as practical approximations.
</Note>

### Which Nisab to Use: Gold or Silver?

This is one of the most important practical questions in zakat calculation, and scholars differ on it.

**The majority view (Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali):** A Muslim should use the silver nisab because it is lower and thus more protective of the rights of the poor. Since silver is much cheaper than gold per gram, the silver nisab results in a lower monetary threshold. This means more people will be obligated to pay zakat, which benefits the poor.

**The Hanafi view:** A Muslim may use either the gold nisab or the silver nisab, whichever is more advantageous to the poor. However, the dominant opinion in the Hanafi school today is to use the silver nisab for the same reason: it is safer for the poor and ensures more people pay.

**Practical recommendation:** Because the price of silver is significantly lower than gold, the silver nisab is the threshold used by most zakat organizations and calculators today. A Muslim whose total zakatable assets exceed the value of 595 grams of silver must pay zakat. You can check current prices and calculate your zakat using our [Zakat Calculator](/tools/zakat-calculator).

### Why the Nisab Matters

The nisab ensures that zakat is only taken from those who have surplus wealth beyond their basic needs. It protects the poor and middle class from being burdened with an obligation they cannot afford. The fact that nisab is tied to precious metals rather than paper currency also provides a stable benchmark that resists inflation over centuries.

## Zakatable Assets

Zakat is due on specific categories of wealth that are capable of growth and accumulation. The major schools of Islamic law agree on the following zakatable assets, though there are some differences in detail.

### 1. Cash and Bank Balances

All forms of money are zakatable: cash on hand, checking accounts, savings accounts, and foreign currency holdings. This is the most common form of wealth for most Muslims today.

### 2. Gold and Silver

Jewelry, coins, bars, and any items made of gold or silver are zakatable at their weight value. This includes investment gold and silver, as well as personal jewelry in most opinions (see the section on jewelry below).

### 3. Trade Goods and Business Inventory

Any goods held for the purpose of trade and resale are zakatable at their current market value. This includes merchandise in a store, products in a warehouse, and raw materials intended for sale.

### 4. Shares, Stocks, and Investments

Shares in companies are zakatable, but scholars differ on how to calculate them:

- **Majority view:** Calculate zakat on the total market value of the shares held, because shares represent ownership in the company's assets.
- **Alternative view:** If the shareholder has no control over the company's assets, some scholars hold that only the liquid assets and cash portion of the company should be considered, which requires examining the company's balance sheet.

For most individual investors, calculating zakat on the total market value of shares is the simpler and safer method. For retirement accounts and pension funds, scholars differ on whether zakat is due annually or only when the funds become accessible. Consult a knowledgeable scholar for your specific situation.

### 5. Livestock

Cattle, sheep, goats, and camels are zakatable if they reach certain thresholds. The rates vary by animal type and number. For example:

- **Camels:** Zakat is due starting at 5 camels, with the rate increasing in brackets.
- **Cattle:** Zakat is due starting at 30 cattle.
- **Sheep and goats:** Zakat is due starting at 40 animals.

The specific rates are detailed in classical fiqh texts and hadith collections including Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim.

### 6. Agricultural Produce

Crops and fruits are zakatable when they reach a minimum threshold (nisab) of approximately 653 kilograms (or about 5 wasq). The rate depends on how the crops are irrigated:

- **5% (one twentieth):** For crops irrigated by natural rainfall, rivers, or without artificial effort.
- **10% (one tenth):** For crops irrigated by artificial means such as wells, pumps, or manual labor.

This is based on the hadith narrated in Sahih al-Bukhari (1483) where the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "On that which is watered by the sky or springs or its own roots, one tenth (is due). And on that which is watered by irrigation, half of one tenth (is due)."

<Note>
  Agricultural zakat is due at harvest time, not after a full lunar year. This is because crops are perishable and seasonal. The nisab for crops is based on volume (wasq) rather than value, and the rate is applied to the total harvest, not just the surplus.
</Note>

### 7. Rental Income and Business Profits

Income from rental properties and business profits that are saved and accumulated are zakatable if they join the rest of the zakatable wealth and the total reaches nisab.

### 8. Debts Owed to You

Money that others owe you and that you expect to recover is generally considered zakatable wealth. Scholars differ on whether the debt is zakatable immediately or only when it is actually received. The stronger view is that strong, expected debts are included in the zakat calculation.

## What Is Not Zakatable

Not all assets and possessions require zakat. The following are generally exempt:

### 1. Personal Residence and Household Items

The house you live in, your furniture, clothing, car for personal use, electronics, and appliances are not zakatable. These are considered necessities of life, not surplus wealth.

### 2. Debts You Owe to Others

Personal debts and loans that you owe are deducted from your total zakatable assets (see below for details on deducting debts).

### 3. Tools of Trade

Equipment, machinery, and tools used to earn a living are not zakatable in themselves, though the profits they generate are zakatable.

### 4. Retirement Accounts (Scholarly Differences)

Scholars differ on whether retirement and pension accounts are zakatable annually or only when the funds become accessible. Some hold that zakat is due annually on the accessible portion; others hold that zakat is only due when the money is withdrawn. Consult a knowledgeable scholar for guidance.

### 5. Jewelry for Personal Use (Scholarly Differences)

This is a significant area of scholarly difference:

- **The Hanafi school:** Jewelry made of gold or silver that is held for personal use and decoration is zakatable if it exceeds what is considered customary and reasonable. If the jewelry is within the bounds of what is normal for a woman of her social standing, some Hanafi scholars exempt it, while others say all gold and silver jewelry is zakatable regardless of use.
- **The majority view (Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali):** Jewelry held for personal use and decoration is not zakatable, because it is considered a necessity or a customary possession like clothing. However, if jewelry is held primarily as an investment or store of value, it is zakatable.

<Info>
  Given the scholarly differences, the safer approach for Muslims is to calculate zakat on all gold and silver jewelry, or at least on the portion that exceeds customary amounts. Our [Zakat Calculator](/tools/zakat-calculator) allows you to include or exclude personal jewelry based on your madhhab preference.
</Info>

## Deducting Debts

When calculating zakat, you may deduct certain debts from your total zakatable assets. However, scholars differ on which debts are deductible and how.

### Immediate Debts

Debts that are due immediately or in the very near term (such as credit card balances, utility bills, and rent due now) are generally deducted in full by all schools.

### Long-Term Debts

For long-term debts such as mortgages, student loans, and car loans, scholarly opinions differ:

- **Hanafi school:** Only the amount currently due (the next payment or the current month's portion) is deducted. The remaining long-term debt is not deducted because it is not yet payable.
- **Some contemporary scholars:** The entire debt may be deducted if the debtor is genuinely burdened by it, because the spirit of zakat is to tax surplus wealth, and a person deep in debt has no true surplus.

<Note>
  The most cautious approach is to deduct only immediate and short-term debts, and to consult a knowledgeable scholar about long-term debts. Many zakat calculators default to deducting only the current year's worth of debt payments or the immediate balance.
</Note>

### Debts Owed to You

Money that others owe you is treated as part of your wealth if you expect to collect it. If the debt is doubtful or the debtor is known to be insolvent, some scholars allow excluding it from the calculation until it is actually received.

## Step-by-Step Zakat Calculation

Calculating zakat involves four main steps: determining your zakatable assets, deducting allowable debts, checking against nisab, and applying the 2.5% rate.

### The Formula

```text
Zakat = (Total Zakatable Assets - Deductible Debts) x 2.5%
```

If the result of (Total Assets - Debts) is equal to or greater than the nisab value, zakat is obligatory.

### Worked Example

The following example uses clearly hypothetical prices for illustration only. You must use current market prices when calculating your own zakat.

**Hypothetical prices (for illustration only):**

- Price of gold: \$60 per gram
- Price of silver: \$0.80 per gram
- Nisab (silver): 595g x \$0.80 = \$476
- Nisab (gold): 85g x \$60 = \$5,100

We will use the silver nisab of \$476 for this calculation, as is the majority recommendation.

#### Step 1: List All Zakatable Assets

| Asset Category | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Cash on hand | \$500 |
| Checking account balance | \$1,200 |
| Savings account balance | \$3,000 |
| Gold jewelry (investment) | \$2,000 |
| Silver coins | \$300 |
| Stocks and shares (market value) | \$4,000 |
| Trade goods (inventory for resale) | \$1,500 |
| Money owed to you (expected repayment) | \$800 |
| **Total Zakatable Assets** | **\$13,300** |

#### Step 2: Deduct Allowable Debts

| Debt Category | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Credit card balance (due now) | \$400 |
| Monthly bills due immediately | \$200 |
| Loan from friend (due this month) | \$300 |
| **Total Deductible Debts** | **\$900** |

<Note>
  For this example, we deduct only immediate debts. If you have long-term debts such as a mortgage, consult a scholar about how much to deduct.
</Note>

#### Step 3: Calculate Net Zakatable Wealth

```text
Net Zakatable Wealth = $13,300 - $900 = $12,400
```

#### Step 4: Compare to Nisab

```text
$12,400 (net wealth) > $476 (silver nisab)
```

The net wealth exceeds the nisab threshold, so zakat is obligatory.

#### Step 5: Calculate Zakat Due

```text
Zakat = $12,400 x 0.025 = $310
```

**Total zakat due: \$310**

This amount should be distributed to the eight categories of zakat recipients described below. You can verify this calculation and adjust it for your own assets using our [Zakat Calculator](/tools/zakat-calculator).

## The Eight Categories of Zakat Recipients

Allah specifies in the Quran exactly eight categories of people who are eligible to receive zakat. No one outside these categories may receive zakat funds.

Allah says:

<p className="io-arabic">إِنَّمَا الصَّدَقَاتُ لِلْفُقَرَاءِ وَالْمَسَاكِينِ وَالْعَامِلِينَ عَلَيْهَا وَالْمُؤَلَّفَةِ قُلُوبُهُمْ وَفِي الرِّقَابِ وَالْغَارِمِينَ وَفِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ وَابْنِ السَّبِيلِ ۖ فَرِيضَةً مِّنَ اللَّهِ ۗ وَاللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ</p>

"Zakat expenditures are only for the poor and for the needy and for those employed to collect (zakat) and for bringing hearts together (for Islam) and for freeing captives (or slaves) and for those in debt and for the cause of Allah and for the (stranded) traveler: an obligation (imposed) by Allah. And Allah is Knowing and Wise." (Quran 9:60)

### 1. The Poor (Al-Fuqara)

Those who have little or no wealth and struggle to meet their basic needs. They may have some income but it is insufficient to cover necessities.

### 2. The Needy (Al-Masakin)

Those who are in greater distress than the poor, often described as people who have absolutely nothing and are completely dependent on charity for survival.

<Note>
  Scholars differ on the exact distinction between al-fuqara and al-masakin. Some say the needy are worse off than the poor; others say the poor are those who cannot meet half their needs, while the needy cannot meet any of their needs. In practice, both categories refer to people in genuine financial difficulty.
</Note>

### 3. Zakat Administrators (Al-Amilina Alayha)

Those who are employed by the state or a recognized organization to collect, safeguard, and distribute zakat. They may receive a fair wage from the zakat funds for their work. This category applies when zakat is collected and distributed through an organized system.

### 4. Those Whose Hearts Are to Be Reconciled (Al-Muallafati Qulubuhum)

New Muslims or those inclined toward Islam who may need financial support to strengthen their faith or to encourage them to embrace or remain in Islam. The applicability of this category today is debated among scholars, especially in Muslim-majority countries where Islam is already established.

### 5. Freeing Captives (Fi Al-Riqab)

This originally referred to freeing slaves, which was a major objective of zakat in early Islam. Today, scholars extend this category to:

- Freeing people from unjust imprisonment
- Paying ransom for hostages or kidnapped persons
- Helping people escape from oppressive situations where they are held against their will

### 6. The Debt-Ridden (Al-Gharimin)

People who are in debt due to circumstances beyond their control, such as:

- Debt incurred to meet basic needs for themselves or their dependents
- Debt from helping others
- Debt from a business failure that was not due to negligence or extravagance

The debt must be genuine and the person must not have the means to repay it from their own resources.

### 7. In the Cause of Allah (Fi Sabillilah)

This category supports activities that serve the broader Muslim community and the cause of Islam. Scholars differ on its exact scope:

- **Majority view:** It refers primarily to jihad and military defense of the Muslim community when legitimately declared by a Muslim authority.
- **Broader view:** Some scholars extend this to include Islamic education, building mosques, printing Quran, and other public welfare projects that benefit the Muslim ummah.

<Note>
  There is scholarly debate about whether "fi sabillilah" includes general charitable causes or is restricted to military jihad. Muslims should follow the guidance of their madhhab or consult knowledgeable scholars when directing zakat to this category.
</Note>

### 8. The Wayfarer (Ibn Al-Sabil)

A traveler who is stranded or cut off from their resources while on a journey. Even if the traveler is wealthy at home, if they lack access to their funds while traveling, they may receive zakat to complete their journey safely.

## Who Cannot Receive Zakat

Just as the Quran specifies who can receive zakat, Islamic law also specifies who cannot receive it. Giving zakat to an ineligible recipient invalidates the obligation, and the giver may need to pay it again to a valid recipient.

### 1. The Wealthy

A person whose wealth exceeds their basic needs and who has surplus above the nisab cannot receive zakat.

### 2. The Giver's Dependents

A person cannot give zakat to those they are legally obligated to support, including:

- Parents and grandparents
- Children and grandchildren
- Spouse (husband or wife)

This is because zakat is meant to help those outside one's immediate financial responsibility. Supporting dependents is already an obligation from one's general wealth, not from zakat.

### 3. Non-Muslims

Zakat is specifically an institution for Muslims. Non-Muslims may receive voluntary charity (sadaqah) and other forms of assistance, but not zakat. This is based on the understanding that zakat is a right of the poor within the Muslim community.

<Note>
  Some contemporary scholars argue that in multicultural societies, zakat may be given to non-Muslims in the category of "those whose hearts are to be reconciled" if there is a clear benefit to the Muslim community. However, the mainstream view remains that zakat recipients should be Muslim.
</Note>

### 4. The Giver Themselves

A person cannot give zakat to themselves, nor can they direct it in a way that primarily benefits their own business or investments.

### 5. Descendants of the Prophet Muhammad (Banu Hashim)

The family of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), specifically the Banu Hashim and Banu Muttalib, are prohibited from receiving zakat. This is out of honor for the Prophet and to ensure that his family is supported through other means. They may receive voluntary charity and other gifts, but not zakat.

## Timing and Paying Zakat Early

### When Is Zakat Due?

Zakat becomes due after a full lunar year (hawl) has passed since the wealth first reached the nisab threshold. Many Muslims choose a fixed date each year (such as Ramadan or the first of Muharram) to review and pay their zakat, making it easier to track.

If you are paying zakat for the first time, the due date is one lunar year from the date your wealth first reached nisab.

### Can Zakat Be Paid Early?

Yes, zakat may be paid in advance before the hawl is completed. The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) accepted zakat payments in advance from some companions.

Paying zakat early is permissible and even recommended if:

- You are certain that your wealth will remain above nisab until the end of the hawl
- You want to take advantage of Ramadan, when good deeds are multiplied
- You want to ensure the poor receive help at a critical time

If you pay early and your wealth later falls below nisab before the hawl completes, the payment is still valid as voluntary charity (sadaqah), and you would need to reassess your zakat obligation.

### Paying Zakat in Installments

While zakat should ideally be paid in full when it becomes due, it is permissible to pay it in installments over the year if necessary. Some Muslims set up monthly zakat payments to charities to make the obligation more manageable. The key is ensuring the full amount is paid within the lunar year.

## Zakat al-Fitr vs Zakat al-Mal

Zakat al-fitr and zakat al-mal are both obligatory forms of charity, but they differ in purpose, timing, amount, and who must pay.

| Feature | Zakat al-Mal | Zakat al-Fitr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Also called** | Zakat on wealth | Fitrana, Sadaqat al-Fitr |
| **Who pays** | Muslims with wealth above nisab | Every Muslim, even the poor (though they may pay on behalf of their household) |
| **What is paid** | 2.5% of surplus wealth | A fixed amount of staple food or its cash equivalent |
| **When paid** | After a full lunar year of owning nisab | Before Eid al-Fitr prayer, at the end of Ramadan |
| **Purpose** | Purify wealth and help the poor year-round | Purify the fasting person from minor sins and help the poor celebrate Eid |
| **Recipients** | The eight categories in Quran 9:60 | The poor and needy |

### Zakat al-Fitr Details

Zakat al-fitr is due on every member of a Muslim household, including children and dependents. The head of the household may pay on behalf of everyone.

The amount is approximately one sa' of staple food per person. A sa' is approximately 2.5 to 3 kilograms. In many countries, Muslims pay the cash equivalent, which is calculated based on the local price of staple food such as wheat, barley, dates, or raisins.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "The Messenger of Allah enjoined zakat al-fitr... to be paid before the people go out to the prayer." (Sahih al-Bukhari)

It is important to pay zakat al-fitr before the Eid prayer so that the poor have food to celebrate the day. Our [Islamic Calendar](/islamic-calendar) can help you track when Ramadan and Eid al-Fitr occur each year.

## Sadaqah vs Zakat

While zakat is obligatory and governed by specific rules, sadaqah (voluntary charity) is broader and more flexible.

### Zakat

- Obligatory (fard) on those who meet the conditions
- Fixed rate (2.5% for most assets, 5% or 10% for crops)
- Specific eligible recipients (Quran 9:60)
- Must reach nisab threshold
- Due after a lunar year

### Sadaqah

- Voluntary (mustahabb / recommended)
- Any amount, large or small
- Can be given to anyone in need, Muslim or non-Muslim
- No minimum threshold
- Can be given at any time

The Prophet (peace be upon him) encouraged sadaqah even in small amounts. He said: "Charity does not decrease wealth." (Sahih Muslim 2588)

Sadaqah also includes non-monetary acts of kindness, such as smiling, helping others, and removing harm from the road. The Prophet said: "Every good deed is charity." (Sahih al-Bukhari)

<Info>
  Both zakat and sadaqah are essential to the spiritual and social fabric of Islam. Zakat ensures a systematic redistribution of wealth, while sadaqah cultivates generosity and compassion in everyday life.
</Info>

## Common Mistakes in Zakat

Many Muslims make errors in calculating or distributing zakat. Here are the most common mistakes to avoid:

### 1. Not Paying Zakat at All

Some Muslims neglect zakat entirely, either from ignorance or procrastination. This is a serious matter, as zakat is one of the five pillars of Islam. The Prophet (peace be upon him) warned about the consequences of withholding zakat.

### 2. Using the Wrong Nisab

Using the gold nisab when the silver nisab is more appropriate (or vice versa) can lead to underpayment or overpayment. Most scholars recommend using the silver nisab because it is lower and benefits more poor people. Use our [Zakat Calculator](/tools/zakat-calculator) to ensure accuracy.

### 3. Forgetting to Include All Assets

Many Muslims only consider cash and forget about:

- Gold and silver jewelry
- Money in multiple bank accounts
- Investments and shares
- Money owed to them by others
- Business inventory

Make a comprehensive list of all your assets before calculating.

### 4. Deducting Non-Deductible Debts

Some people deduct long-term mortgage balances or future debts that are not yet due. The safer approach is to deduct only immediate and short-term debts. Consult a scholar about long-term debts.

### 5. Giving Zakat to Ineligible Recipients

Giving zakat to wealthy relatives, non-Muslims (in the majority view), or the giver's own dependents invalidates the obligation. Ensure your zakat goes to one of the eight categories specified in Quran 9:60.

### 6. Confusing Zakat al-Mal with Zakat al-Fitr

These are separate obligations with different rules. Zakat al-fitr is due on every household member at the end of Ramadan, regardless of wealth. Zakat al-mal is due annually on surplus wealth above nisab.

### 7. Delaying Payment Without Reason

Zakat should be paid promptly when it becomes due. Unnecessary delay is discouraged, especially when the poor are in immediate need.

### 8. Not Accounting for Fluctuating Wealth

If your wealth fluctuates above and below nisab during the year, the hawl may be interrupted. Track your wealth carefully, especially if you have irregular income or large expenses.

## Frequently Asked Questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I have to pay zakat on my house and car?">
    No. Your personal residence and personal vehicle are not zakatable because they are necessities of life, not surplus wealth. However, if you own additional properties or vehicles for investment or rental purposes, the profits from them are zakatable, and some scholars hold that the property itself may be zakatable if held purely for resale.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I pay zakat on my retirement account or pension?">
    Scholars differ on this. Some hold that zakat is due annually on the accessible balance of a retirement account. Others hold that zakat is only due when the funds are withdrawn and come under your full control. The safest approach is to consult a knowledgeable scholar who understands your specific retirement plan and jurisdiction. If you choose to pay annually, calculate zakat on the current balance minus any penalties for early withdrawal.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What if my wealth drops below nisab during the year?">
    If your wealth drops below nisab at any point during the lunar year, the hawl is interrupted. A new hawl begins when your wealth reaches nisab again. However, if the drop is only temporary (for example, you withdraw money and redeposit it shortly after), some scholars hold that the hawl continues if the wealth was never truly separated from your ownership.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I give zakat to my poor relatives?">
    Yes, you may give zakat to poor relatives as long as they are not your direct dependents (parents, children, spouse) and as long as they are Muslim and eligible. In fact, giving zakat to relatives is encouraged because it combines charity with maintaining family ties. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Charity given to a poor person is charity, and charity given to a relative is two things: charity and maintaining family ties." (Sunan al-Tirmidhi)
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is zakat due on cryptocurrency and digital assets?">
    Cryptocurrency held as an investment or store of value is zakatable according to most contemporary scholars, because it functions as a form of wealth. Calculate zakat on the current market value of your cryptocurrency holdings at the 2.5% rate, provided the total zakatable wealth (including crypto) reaches nisab. Cryptocurrency used for daily transactions and not held as wealth may be treated differently.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I pay zakat through a charity organization?">
    Yes, it is permissible and often practical to pay zakat through a recognized Islamic charity or zakat organization. Ensure that the organization is trustworthy, distributes zakat according to the Quranic categories, and does not mix zakat funds with general charity funds. Many organizations provide zakat receipts and ensure proper distribution to eligible recipients worldwide.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I pay zakat on money I lent to others?">
    If you have a strong expectation of repayment, the debt owed to you is part of your zakatable wealth. Include it in your calculation. If the debt is doubtful or the debtor is unlikely to pay, some scholars allow you to exclude it and pay zakat on it only when you actually receive it. The stronger view is to include expected debts.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is the difference between nisab for gold and silver, and which should I use?">
    The gold nisab is approximately 85 grams of pure gold, while the silver nisab is approximately 595 grams of pure silver. Because silver is much cheaper per gram, the silver nisab results in a lower monetary threshold. The majority of scholars recommend using the silver nisab because it is more protective of the poor and ensures more people fulfill the obligation. Our [Zakat Calculator](/tools/zakat-calculator) uses the silver nisab by default but allows you to adjust.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Conclusion

Zakat is far more than a tax or a financial obligation. It is a pillar of Islam that purifies the soul, cleanses wealth, and builds a just society where the needs of the poor are met with dignity. By giving 2.5% of surplus wealth annually, Muslims fulfill a command of Allah, follow the Sunnah of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), and contribute to the well-being of the ummah.

Understanding the conditions of zakat, the nisab threshold, zakatable assets, and the proper recipients ensures that this obligation is fulfilled correctly and sincerely. In a world of growing inequality, zakat remains a divinely ordained mechanism for wealth redistribution that predates modern social welfare systems by over fourteen centuries.
